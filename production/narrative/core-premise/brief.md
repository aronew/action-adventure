# Core Lore Premise: Three Candidates

Status: PROPOSAL for owner pick. None is developed. Each is one premise; everything else must grow from it.
Author: covering narrative-director. Nothing here is canon until chosen.

---

## A. The Lamp Stair

**Premise.** A mountain city kept one great lamp burning to hold off a dark, and the tenders who kept it did so by pouring themselves into the flame. The lamp is almost out, and the city has been climbing toward its last light for a very long time.

1. **Seeker.** The last of the tenders' errand-runners, sent down the stair to relight the stations. Hooded against glare; bone-pale from a life lived out of the sun.
2. **Guard.** Borrowed flame. Tenders who gave themselves to the lamp carry a shell of its light; ordinary creatures never tended it, so they have none. The charged heavy floods the blade with light until it matches the shell, which then has nowhere to hold it and gives way.
3. **Keepers.** Station tenders who would not let their lamp go out, and so cannot stop. Beaten, a Keeper hands over the station's key. Each key is a way of crossing the stair (dash, grapple), because a tender's craft was getting light from one place to the next.
4. **Shrines / mentor.** Shrines are dark stations with a tool left on the sill. The mentor is a retired tender in a hub who teaches the double jump in exchange for a promise.
5. **Save point.** A lantern the Seeker relights. Shortcuts are the stair folding back to it.
6. **Palettes.** Void, Ember and Frost are different fuels for the same flame. Dream Seeker: left open.
7. **Tone / image.** Hushed, warm, failing. The Seeker's swallowed blade is the only light in a black hall, and it shows a painted ceiling nobody has seen in years.
8. **Risks.** Light against dark is the most generic axis available. It only works if the lamp is never a "good" force: it is a habit the city cannot quit.

## B. The Sleepers' Ward

**Premise.** When the end came slowly, the city chose to sleep through it, and a few stayed awake to keep the others asleep. Nothing is coming to wake them, and the watchers have not noticed.

1. **Seeker.** A waker: something the ward made to walk among the sleepers without joining them. Hooded and bone-pale like a burial cowl and shroud.
2. **Guard.** A held dream. Watchers keep the sleepers' dreams in place by wearing one as armour; ordinary creatures are only the leftover waking things and have none. The charged heavy is dawn in the blade, light that the dream cannot absorb.
3. **Keepers.** Watchers who stayed awake and have hardened around the duty. Each holds a way of moving the sealed city forgot. Beaten, a Keeper stops resisting and the Seeker is allowed to keep that motion.
4. **Shrines / mentor.** Shrines are sleeper-alcoves where someone left a body habit behind. The mentor is the one watcher who has begun to doubt, a hub figure who teaches the double jump.
5. **Save point.** A cot with a folded blanket. The Seeker lies down; the ward goes quiet around it.
6. **Palettes.** Kinds of dream: Void (dreamless), Ember (fever), Frost (stillness). Dream Seeker is the Seeker lucid inside the ward's dream, which fits unusually well.
7. **Tone / image.** Tender, uncanny, slow. A long hall of sleepers, each breathing in time, and one empty bed.
8. **Risks.** "It was all a dream" drift. The sleepers must be physically real and the world must not be a dream. Also close to a waking-the-world story, which edges toward a stakes bar we have banned.

## C. The Kiln City (recommended)

**Premise.** A city of potters learned to make things that outlast their makers, then began glazing themselves against the end. The kilns are still warm, and the glazed are still standing.

1. **Seeker.** The city's last made thing, a figure of unglazed bone-clay (bisque), sent to finish what the makers could not: to let the kilns go cold. Bone-pale because it was never fired or glazed. The hood is the unfinished shape of the clay.
2. **Guard.** Glaze. A glazed being is hard, bright and preserved. Ordinary creatures are unglazed, so they chip, weather and die. The charged heavy puts kiln-heat into the blade; the glaze crazes and cracks, and the finisher is a break along the crack.
3. **Keepers.** Master makers who glazed themselves to stay as they were. Each held a craft of passage (courier, climber, rope-hand). Beaten, a Keeper's craft is released to the Seeker as a movement key, because it was the one thing they were still carrying.
4. **Shrines / mentor.** Shrines are abandoned benches with one last piece left unfinished; the Seeker completes the gesture and gains the ability (wall cling). The mentor is a journeyman who never glazed himself, in a hub, and who teaches the double jump in return for a promise, still unstated, about his own end.
5. **Save point.** A cooling niche in a kiln wall. The Seeker rests and anneals slowly. Shortcuts are the flue-ways folding back to it.
6. **Palettes.** Firings and glazes: Bone is bisque, Ember is a hot firing, Frost is a crackle glaze, Void is a black reduction. This is the best palette fit of the three. Dream Seeker: left open (a kiln-glow translucence is an option).
7. **Tone / image.** Dry, tender, brittle. A gallery of glazed figures mid-gesture, glossy and untouched, with the Seeker's strike running one clean crack through the nearest.
8. **Risks.** Pottery metaphor can tip into precious craft-talk; keep it to objects and hands. Also "the end as mercy" can turn into a lecture; the mentor's promise must stay small and specific. Glazing must never become a pantheon of kiln-gods.

---

## Recommendation

Pick **C**. It answers the guard rule most directly: a guard is a preservation, and that is what makes Keepers sad rather than evil. The bone-pale Seeker is the one unfinished thing in a finished world. The four palettes are literally firings. Heat in the blade explains the charged heavy without adding a magic system. It scales to later regions as other kilns and other trades. A is the safest and the most generic; B is the strongest for the Dream Seeker but carries the highest twist and stakes risk.

Open questions for the owner (not blocking): who fired the first glaze, and was that a choice anyone voted for. Deliberately not answered here.
