# Kiln City: Lore Premise

Status: DRAFT for owner approval. Author: covering world-builder. Seed: premise C in `../core-premise/brief.md`.
Canon levels: **Canon** = fixed, other files must obey. **Provisional** = best answer now, owner may change.

## Premise

A city of potters learned to make things that outlast their makers, then began glazing themselves against the end. The kilns are still warm, the glazed are still standing, and the last unglazed thing in the city has been sent to let the fires go out.

## World rules

| Entry | Rule | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Glaze | A fired skin over a body. It seals: no wear, no age, no healing, no change. It is the guard of bosses and mini-bosses. | Canon |
| Why the glazed endure | Glaze holds under slow, even warmth. Nothing gets in, nothing gets out. They stay the hour they were fired. | Canon |
| Crazing | Sudden heat or sudden cold cracks glaze. This is why a charged heavy (the Seeker's one firing poured into the blade) breaks a guard. | Canon |
| The end | The Dry. The clay beds and the kiln-wood are spent. Nothing is coming. Unglazed things wear to dust within a lifetime or two and nothing new can be made. | Canon |
| Who is glazed and who moves | Most of the glazed are still figures, fired at rest or mid-stride. Keepers moved on because they were glazed mid-task; the task keeps them. | Canon |
| The kilns | Ordinary kilns, banked at the last firing and never let out. Their slow heat is what keeps the glaze whole. Cold would craze it too, slowly. | Canon |
| The Seeker | Made by Teodra, last unglazed master of Wheel Row, from clay of the dry bed. Fired once to bisque and deliberately never glazed. Porous, so a Keeper's craft (a habit of the hands) soaks in when the glaze breaks. | Canon |
| Teodra's errand | "A kiln that is never allowed to cool is never unloaded." Let the kilns go cold, so the work can finally be finished. Teodra is dust now. | Canon |
| Wasters | Flawed pieces thrown on the heaps; warmth from the banked kilns keeps them twitching toward the shape they were meant to be. No memory, no glaze. | Provisional |
| Dream Seeker | Not hinted in region 1. Left open. | Open |

## True answers to every hinted mystery

| Mystery | True answer (the player may never be told) | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Who fired the first glaze, and was it chosen? | Hesk, a glaze-mixer, on the last cold night of a bad winter, over her dying daughter Ilo, to hold her until morning. It held. No one chose it for the city: one woman, one child. Households copied it one by one, and by the last evening whole guilds fired together. No council ever voted. Hesk glazed herself beside Ilo and stands in a later region. She is not a final boss. | Canon (location Provisional) |
| What did Teodra want? | To be unloaded, not preserved. She made the Seeker because she was too frightened of the glaze to walk into the kilns herself. | Provisional |
| Why do Keepers fight? | Their last task is still running. Tavik is still arriving. Brun is still stacking. They did not ask for release and cannot say whether they want it. | Canon |
| What does the Seeker take from a Keeper? | The craft of passage, because it was the one thing the Keeper was still carrying. | Canon |
| What is Pell's promise? | Pell, journeyman, never glazed, because he was frightened, not noble. He is wearing down. He asks the Seeker to refuse him when, near his end, he asks to be glazed. In the end he will ask, and the Seeker is to say no and stay while he wears away. Core build never reaches this. | Canon (end scene Provisional) |
| Why did Pell not glaze? | Fear of standing still while the others went on. He told no one. | Canon |
| Where is everyone else? | Dust. Every unglazed person in the city has worn away except Pell. | Canon |
| Does the Seeker succeed? | Left open. No kiln-god, no chosen one: the Seeker is a made thing doing an errand. | Open |

## Banned-trope guard

No pantheon, no prophecy, no world-saving stakes. Glaze has no author beyond Hesk. Kilns are kilns. Pottery words name objects and hands (tray, saggar, stilt, flue, slip), never craft philosophy.
