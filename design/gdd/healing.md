## Summary

Healing is the Seeker's way to win health back mid-fight: every hit lands into a single heal meter, and a full meter can be spent on a 1.0 s planted channel that restores 30% of max health. A hit cancels the channel but keeps the meter, and letting go is always free, so a heal costs time and the damage taken while planted, never the work of earning it. It exists because recovery should be earned by fighting well (pillar 1, anti-pillar 2: no consumables), and because the three-stage boss needs a player who can recover between stages.

## Overview

Healing lets the Seeker recover health in the middle of a fight, and it is earned by fighting. Every hit the Seeker lands fills a **heal meter**; when the meter is full the Seeker can spend it to restore health, as Hollow Knight's Focus does (owner, 2026-11-22). The player pays for a heal with good play (landing hits) and with the second it takes, never with an item, a currency or a trip to a save point. The meter is sized from the heal itself (about 4 fill points per HP restored), so a heal costs the same amount of fighting at every max health. **What the risk is, stated plainly.** Letting go and every movement press cancel for free, the meter is kept on every cancel (owner rules), and at today's boss timings a heal started right after a strike ends 0.6 to 0.9 s before the next wind-up begins. A heal against the current boss therefore costs one second and a hit that lands, and nothing else. **Owner decision, third design review (2026-11-23): the stake belongs to the boss, not to the meter.** Healing keeps its meter rules and **requires** the boss to take that safety away with a pattern or stage element that strikes inside the channel window (Open Question 12, owed to Bosses and listed in its Dependencies). Until that exists, nothing in this document may call a heal a gamble. Without Healing, health is a one-way budget until the next rest, and a long boss fight punishes one early mistake for the whole fight.

**Design intent**
- **Owner, 2026-11-22:** a meter filled by landing hits; the Seeker can heal when it is full, as in Hollow Knight. The exact rules (meter size, gain per hit, heal amount, channel time and the risk while healing, whether hits on a guarded foe count, whether it fills in a boss fight, whether it empties on death) were undecided and are settled in Detailed Design below.
- **Owner, 2026-11-22 (first boss playtest):** the boss was beaten and found "super easy"; the owner wants a three-stage boss with no health bar and a heal meter, and the boss is to be tuned together with healing (`bosses.md`, "Planned: three stages").
- **From the concept (anti-pillar 2):** no loot grind or crafting, so a heal is never a consumable, a purchase or a drop. A meter filled by combat fits that.
- **[Rec]** Pillar 1 (Movement Is the Mastery): recovery is earned by fighting, not by retreating to a save point, which fits the pillar. **A tension kept on purpose:** the meter is fed by any hit (a pogo fills 8, a charged heavy 60), so it does not reward moving through the fight, and the heal itself is a planted second, the opposite of flowing momentum. The planted channel is what makes a heal a decision; the pillar's answer is that every cancel by movement is free (rule 9), so movement is always the way out. The cost of that answer is stated in the Overview: the free cancel is also why the boss, not Healing, has to supply the risk.

**Scope**: in: the heal meter (capacity, how it fills, whether it keeps or empties), the heal action (input, channel, cost, interruption), how it restores health, and the rules for foes and bosses. Out: the health pool, damage and death (R6, code in `player_health.gd`), the meter and health display (HUD, R9), the heal effect and sound (Hit feedback, R8), the max-health upgrades (Collectible upgrades), and the boss's stage tuning (Bosses).

## Player Fantasy

*Advisory at the `standard` tier; added in the fourth design review so the rules have something to be tested against.*

- **Ready.** The player is fighting well and feels the bar fill under their hits. When a heal is usable the Seeker carries a quiet mark: *a breath is banked, and I can take it when I choose.*
- **Committing.** Pressing heal is a decision. The Seeker plants and the light gathers for one second, and the player is reading the boss, not the button: *now is the moment.* Backing out is always allowed, so a misread is never a trap, only a wasted second.
- **Being hit.** If a strike lands, the light breaks and the hit hurts in full. The player keeps the bar they earned and blames the boss's timing, never the rules.
- **What it must never feel like.** A consumable (anti-pillar 2), a lock (any movement press leaves the channel), or a free refill (the health pool must still matter).
- **Test.** AC-26's playtest asks "felt earned", "felt risky" and "felt fair" in these terms.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

Tags: **[Owner]** = the owner's stated rule; **[Rec]** = recommendation, change freely; **[Code]** = how the code works today. `game-designer`, `systems-designer` and `gameplay-programmer` not consulted for this section: Lean mode. Review manually before production. Values named in `code font` are data, set in Formulas.

**The meter**

1. **One bar.** The heal meter is one number, `heal_meter`, from 0 to `meter_capacity`. It is a single bar, not segments: one full bar is one heal. **[Owner, 2026-11-22: a bar, a heal spends it all]**
2. **What fills it.** Every hit the Seeker lands on a foe adds `damage` to the meter (the meter is counted in damage units, so `meter_capacity` (derived from the heal, F1) sets how many hits a heal takes), where `damage` is the value Combat reports in `hit_landed` for that hit (the attack's base damage, with the charged heavy's x2 already in it, before the guarded, finisher and edge multipliers). A hit on a guarded foe or boss fills as much as any other: the guard multiplier does not reduce it. The killing blow counts. **[Owner, 2026-11-22: each hit fills by its attack's base damage; Rec: the charged heavy fills double]**
3. **Only foes feed it.** A hit counts only on a foe's hurtbox. `hit_landed` carries only the `Area2D` and fires for anything in the `hittable` group (the `TrainingDummy` and `HitTarget` test nodes included), so a foe is marked explicitly: the hurtboxes of `EnemyBase` (bosses included) and of the `TrainingDummy` join a new group `foe`, and `HitTarget` does not. The player's `hit_landed` handler fills the meter only when the target is in `foe` (Dependencies). A hit on anything else, such as a pogo on a hazard, a breakable or a pane, adds nothing. A dead foe's hurtbox leaves `hittable` when it dies (enemies rule 7), so it reports nothing. The training dummy feeds the meter on purpose, so the meter can be tried in the test room. **[Rec]**
4. **The meter caps and keeps.** The meter never exceeds `meter_capacity`; the excess of a hit that crosses the cap is lost. It does not decay with time, and it keeps filling while health is full, so a player can arrive at a fight with a heal ready. **[Rec]**
5. **Resets.** The meter is 0 at a new game, after a Continue and after a death (the `revive` call, which also resets the heal timer and phase). A rest at a save point and a room change leave it as it is. It is not saved: like health, it is not in the world-state record (world state rule 1). **[Owner, 2026-11-22: starts empty after a death, a Continue or a new game; a rest leaves it]**

**The heal**

6. **Input.** A new action, `heal`, held to heal (or toggled, rule 14). It starts a heal only when all of these hold on the press: the meter is at `meter_capacity`; **missing health (max health minus health) is at least `start_deficit`** (F2: `max(1, min(start_deficit_share x heal amount, start_deficit_cap))`, so 15 at 100 max health, 20 from about 133 max health up, and never less than 1, so a heal never starts at full health); and the Seeker's state is `GROUND` (the controller states `ATTACK`, `CROUCH`, `HURT` and `DEAD` are separate states, so "on the ground, not attacking, not crouching, not in hurt lock and not dead" is exactly `state == GROUND`; the landing and standing-up animations are flags inside `GROUND`, so a press during them starts the heal at once and cuts the animation). The meter is still spent whole when the heal completes (rule 8), so `start_deficit` only stops a whole bar being burned for a few points of health. Heal start is polled in `_state_ground` after the jump check and before crouch **[Rec]**, so a jump press on the same tick wins, and a heal cannot start on a tick on which a heal ended (rule 9). If the press fails, nothing starts and the player gets a refusal cue by reason (UI Requirements). **When several reasons apply, one cue plays, chosen in this priority: a movement or combat state first (silent, because the reason is visible on the Seeker), then the meter not full, then missing health below `start_deficit`.** A press in the air with the meter below capacity gives no cue; a press on the ground with meter 50 and health 100 gives the meter cue only. **[Rec: ground only, so a heal is a planted commitment and pillar 1's movement is not blocked mid-air; Owner, 2026-11-22: one bar spent whole, kept]**
7. **The channel.** A started heal is a channel of `channel_time` (1.0 s), run as the new top-level controller state `Player.State.HEAL` (this document's `CHANNEL`, Dependencies). The Seeker is planted: it skids to a stop on a `heal_deceleration` of its own (data, defaults to the charge skid's 2000 px/s², so Healing does not read the combat config) and cannot attack, jump, dash or grapple except to cancel (rule 9). If it slides off an edge during the skid, the channel is cancelled as an involuntary cancel (rule 9). The timer accumulates the `delta` of the Seeker's own `_physics_process` (it is pausable and game-time: a pause freezes it, hit-stop stretches it); no scene-tree timer and no real-time clock may drive it, because those keep running while paused. The glow and pose read `timer / channel_time`, not a separate tween. Like `GROUND`, `HEAL` refreshes coyote time and the air moves every tick and keeps gravity applied to `velocity.y` (as `_state_ground` does), and an edge is detected by `not is_on_floor()` after `move_and_slide`, so a slide off an edge still gives a coyote jump. **[Owner, 2026-11-22: 1.0 s channel, planted]**
8. **Completion.** When the channel timer reaches `channel_time` (tested as `timer >= channel_time - 0.0001`, so it completes on physics tick 60 and never before it; tick 0 is the physics tick on which a press starts the channel with the timer at 0, and the timer after tick N is N x `delta`) with the heal still held (in toggle mode: not cancelled) **and health below max health**, the meter is spent in full (set to 0) and health rises by the heal amount (Formulas F2), never above max health. If health is already at max health at that moment (a health step refilled it mid-channel, Edge Cases), nothing is spent and nothing is healed: the meter is kept. Nothing is spent before that moment. The completion step runs after every `take_hit` caller has acted on that tick (rule 15), and tests that the state is still `HEAL`. **[Owner, 2026-11-22: the meter is spent only when the channel completes]**
9. **Cancelling.** The channel ends with nothing spent and no heal, the meter kept in every case, and the time already channelled is lost (no partial heal, no progress kept). **No cancel has a recovery or a penalty: letting go is always free** **[Owner, 2026-11-23, fourth design review: the late-release recovery and the free window are cut for the first build; Open Question 22 says when to bring one back]**.
    - **Involuntary** (control goes where the event sends it): the Seeker takes a hit that lands (a hit in the invulnerability window does not land, so it does not cancel) and goes to HURT or DEAD; the Seeker dies; a door, an exit or a rest takes over (`interact` with a door or rest area overlapped); the Seeker slides off an edge (rule 7) and goes to AIR.
    - **Voluntary, by a movement or attack press** (free): the Seeker presses jump, dash, grapple (once owned) or attack. The channel ends on that tick: `_state_heal` calls `_set_state(GROUND)` and then `_state_ground(delta)` on the same tick, so the pressed action starts as it would in `GROUND`. Jump, dash and grapple start that tick; **attack arms Combat's own tap/hold resolution that tick** (a tap swings on release, a hold becomes the heavy). Detection is `Input.is_action_just_pressed` on that tick, never the jump buffer (a jump pressed up to 0.12 s before the heal started would otherwise cancel it on its first tick). The jump buffer is zeroed when the channel starts and on every exit **except a jump cancel**, whose dispatched `_state_ground` consumes it as it always does, so the jump that caused the cancel is not eaten. When several presses arrive on one tick the one `_state_ground` would pick wins, in its polling order: grapple, dash, door or rest `interact`, attack, jump.
    - **Voluntary, by letting go:** the heal input is released (hold mode, detected as `not Input.is_action_pressed`, rule 14) or pressed a second time (toggle mode). The Seeker is in `GROUND` on that tick as if no heal had been pressed. A release on the completion tick does not cancel: the heal completes (rule 8, rule 15), and in toggle mode so does a second press on that tick.
    - **Everything else is ignored and not buffered:** crouch, climb, `interact` with no door or rest area, bow, cast, rapid fire, map, stick or D-pad movement. A jump press always cancels (above), so none is ever buffered during a channel, and the jump buffer is zeroed on every exit but a jump cancel, so a press made during the channel never fires after it.
    - **A press is spent once.** A `heal` press that starts a heal or cancels one (the toggle's second press) is consumed: `_heal_press_age` is cleared on a start and on every exit, and a heal that ends on a tick cannot start again on that tick. A second press therefore cannot cancel and restart in one tick, and a button still held after a dash or attack cancel does not restart a heal (rule 14's fresh-press rule).
    **[Owner, 2026-11-22: a hit cancels, meter kept; Rec: the movement presses cancel so the player is never stuck for 1.0 s; Owner, 2026-11-23: letting go is always free, so a slip costs nothing and the stake is the boss's (Overview)]**
10. **No protection of its own.** The channel gives no invulnerability, no damage reduction and no armour. A hit that lands cancels it and deals its full damage, and starts the controller's hurt lock and invulnerability as any hit does. **One accepted exception (owner, 2026-11-23, fourth design review):** a heal that starts while the invulnerability window of an earlier hit is still running (the window outlasts the 0.35 s hurt lock by 0.65 s) is protected from hits until that window ends, because a hit in the window does not land (rule 9; AC-17b). It is kept because the 0.12 s press buffer after `HURT` makes right after a hit the natural moment to heal, and it is a stage-tuning constraint: a later boss pattern must strike after the window to punish such a heal. **[Rec: a hit cancels so it cannot be tanked through. Against the current boss the risk is only the second spent and a hit that lands (rule 12, Open Question 12)]**
11. **Fills do not happen during a heal.** The Seeker cannot attack while channelling, so nothing fills the meter then. A hit landed by a delayed source (none exist today) would still count: the fill is not refused while the state is `HEAL` (AC-34). **[Rec]**

**Boss fights**

12. **The meter fills in a boss fight like anywhere else** (rule 2). A boss fight is where it matters most, so the boss's stages are tuned with the heal in mind. The starting values are **not** tuned to a heals-per-cycle target. Formulas F3 bounds what a skilled player can earn per guard-break cycle (1 heal in the stagger; 2 when a banked heal is also spent in the guard phase; about 1.5 sustained on the charged-heavy break path, F3's cap-aware model), and whether that is fair is **decided in play** from a log of HP healed against boss damage taken per cycle (AC-26); the boss's damage is tuned against what the playtests show. **The windows a heal fits in:** the gap from the end of one boss strike to the start of the next wind-up is the previous pattern's recovery plus `attack_cooldown` 0.6 s: 1.9 s after a slam, 1.6 s after a lunge or shockwave. The next strike comes that gap plus the next pattern's wind-up later, and the pattern just used is not picked again (`bosses.md` rule 3), so on pattern arithmetic alone the gap to the next strike is **at least 2.4 s** (2.4 to 3.0 s), and 1.0 s more after a stagger. These are **lower bounds**: the pattern choice also depends on distance (`bosses.md`, Pattern choice: slam within 64 px, lunge within 200 px, shockwave beyond 220 px), so for a Seeker planted in one place some pairs cannot follow each other (after a slam only the lunge can) and the boss's walk to the next range (chase speed 55 px/s) adds time. A 1.0 s channel started right after a strike therefore **ends at least 0.6 to 0.9 s before the next wind-up begins**, and the boss cannot punish it at the current timings. That safety is the boss's to take away (Open Question 12, a requirement since the owner decision in the Overview). **One exception:** a shockwave already in flight (3.5 s at 150 px/s) reaches a Seeker planted at distance d from its release after d / 150 s, so a heal started under a live wave is only safe if the wave has passed or will not arrive within the channel. **In a stagger (4.0 s)** a heal costs 1.0 s of punish damage on the boss's health, but the planned stages end on a guard-break count (`bosses.md`), so until the last stage that damage is not what progress needs and a heal in a stagger is nearly free (owner-accepted, watched in the AC-26 log). **[Owner, 2026-11-22: tune the boss together with healing; the numbers cannot say whether it feels balanced or fair, so some of these values are determined by play]**
13. **A stage change neither fills nor empties the meter.** What the stage transition does to the player's heal is not decided here; see Open Questions.
14. **Input options: press buffer and hold or toggle.** *Press buffer:* a `heal` press that fails only because the Seeker is in `ATTACK`, `HURT` or `AIR` (a state that ends on its own) is remembered for `press_buffer` (0.12 s). If every start condition of rule 6 holds within that window and the input is still held, the heal starts on that tick; the test is `press age <= press_buffer`, so a `press_buffer` of 0 still starts a heal on the press tick. A press that fails on the meter or the health (rule 6) is never buffered, so a press just before the meter fills starts nothing. *Hold or toggle:* an accessibility option `heal_mode` in the options (Pause and menus owns the page, Open Question 14): in `hold` mode (default) the channel needs the input held and releasing cancels (rule 9); in `toggle` mode a press starts the channel, it runs without being held, and a second press cancels it as a release does. Every other cancel and the completion are the same in both modes. Toggle mode must ship with the heal, because controls cannot be remapped in the first scope (`pause-and-menus.md` rule 17). *Input detection:* a start and a buffered press use `Input.is_action_just_pressed`; a release in hold mode is detected by `not Input.is_action_pressed`, never by `just_released`, because a release that happens while the game is paused is lost as an edge and a held-state poll is the one that resumes correctly; the Seeker tracks `_heal_press_age` (seconds since the press, advanced by the physics `delta`, cleared when the press is spent, rule 9) for the fresh-press rule; a press that fails to start does not arm the toggle; **a `heal` press made on the first tick after the game resumes from a pause is not a heal press** (polling `Input` cannot tell whether the pause menu consumed it, so the Seeker ignores it); and a tap shorter than one physics tick is not guaranteed to register (unverified against the engine, checked in the first story). **[Rec, second design review; third and fourth design reviews: input detection and the spent press]**
15. **Order within a physics tick.** The hitters act first, then the Seeker, so a hit that lands on a tick always wins over a heal that would complete on that tick. Tick 0 is the press tick: the Seeker enters `HEAL` with the timer at 0 and no advance. On every later tick the heal logic runs in this order: (a) a landed hit this tick has already cancelled the channel (rule 9), so a channel found in `HEAL` has not been hit; (b) a cancel press is applied, and a release or a toggle's second press is applied **unless this is the completion tick**, meaning `timer + delta` reaches `channel_time - 0.0001` (step (b) reads the timer before it advances, so it looks one `delta` ahead); (c) the timer advances by `delta`; (d) completion is tested (rule 8). **The tick-60 asymmetry is on purpose:** a release or a second press on the completion tick completes the heal, and a jump, dash, grapple or attack press on it cancels and the heal is lost (step (b)). The mechanism that makes hitters act first (the Seeker's `process_physics_priority` and the nodes it must follow) is in Dependencies, Implementation notes. **[Rec, second design review; third and fourth design reviews: the order, the lookahead and the asymmetry]**

### States and Transitions

The meter has no states of its own: it is a number, and "full" means `heal_meter >= meter_capacity`. The heal action is one new top-level controller state, `Player.State.HEAL`, appended to the `Player.State` enum with its own `_state_heal` (the existing `GROUND` handler polls grapple, dash, interact, combat, climb, the jump buffer and crouch before it moves, so a sub-state of `GROUND` would need a guard on every one of them). It has one phase, the channel (`CHANNEL` below); `IDLE` means the Seeker is not in `HEAL`. Cleanup (timer reset, effects off) runs in `_set_state` on any exit from `HEAL`, and the jump buffer is zeroed on entry and on every exit except a jump cancel (rule 9). (Dependencies)

| Phase | Meaning | Enters when | Leaves when |
|-------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| `IDLE` | No heal in progress (the Seeker is not in `HEAL`) | Start of play; a heal completes or is cancelled | A fresh heal press (or a buffered press, rule 14) and rule 6 holds: to `CHANNEL` |
| `CHANNEL` | Planted, timer running from 0 to `channel_time` | Rule 6 holds on a press | The timer reaches `channel_time` (tick 60) with the heal held (hold mode) or not cancelled (toggle mode): to `IDLE` after the heal and the spend, or with nothing spent when health is already at max (rule 8). A jump, dash, grapple or attack press: to `IDLE` and the pressed action starts (rule 9). Letting go (hold mode) or a second press (toggle mode) before the completion tick: to `IDLE` at once. A hit, death, door or rest, or a slide off an edge: to `IDLE` and the event's state (rule 9) |

Meter and health outcomes of the exits:

| Exit | Meter | Health | Player state after |
|------|-------|--------|--------------------|
| Completes (health below max) | 0 | + heal amount (capped) | GROUND, control returns the same tick |
| Completes with health already at max | kept | unchanged | GROUND, control returns the same tick |
| Letting go, or a second press in toggle mode | kept | unchanged | GROUND, control the same tick |
| Jump, dash, grapple or attack pressed | kept | unchanged | the pressed action starts the same tick (attack: Combat's tap/hold resolution is armed) |
| A hit lands | kept | - the hit's damage | HURT (or DEAD) |
| Slide off an edge | kept | unchanged | AIR |
| Door or rest takes over | kept | unchanged | ENTER_DOOR or the rest |
| Death | 0 on revive | 0 | DEAD |

## Formulas

Values are data (a `HealingConfig` resource, e.g. `src/player/healing_config.tres` **[Rec]**), never constants in code. The figures they act on come from `attack_data.tres` (damage 8-30, charged heavy x2), `player_config.tres` (`max_health` 100, up to 180 with collectible upgrades) and the enemy and boss `.tres` files. Starting values approved by the owner on 2026-11-22. The `systems-designer` lens was applied in-session (lean mode spawns no agent); every variable was worked at its minimum, its maximum and 0.

### F1. Meter fill

The meter_fill and meter_capacity formulas are defined as:

`heal_meter' = min(heal_meter + max(damage, 0), meter_capacity)`

`meter_capacity = max(capacity_floor, meter_cost_ratio x heal_share x max_health)`

When max health changes (a collectible step completes), the meter keeps its fraction: `heal_meter' = heal_meter x new_capacity / old_capacity`, so a full bar stays full and a half bar stays half **[Rec, fourth design review; owner decision of 2026-11-23 for the derived capacity]**.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| heal_meter | float | 0 to `meter_capacity` | calculated | The meter before the hit; 0 at a new game, a Continue and a death |
| damage | float | 8-60 | calculated (Combat `hit_landed`) | The hit's base damage: `attack_data.tres` damage, with the charged heavy's x2, before the guarded, finisher and edge multipliers |
| meter_capacity | float | 84 to 720 (derived) | calculated | The meter value at which a heal is ready: 120 at 100 max health and the defaults, 216 at 180. Derived, not a constant: the registry's 120 is the result at 100 max health |
| meter_cost_ratio | float | 2.8-10, starts at 4.0 | data file | Fill points per HP restored. At 4.0 a heal costs the same fighting at every max health (0.25 HP per fill point; with a fixed 120 it was 0.45 at 180 max health and 0.70 at 280). 2.8 is the old minimum capacity of 84 at the defaults: below it one combo plus a hit fills the bar and the loop is undamped; above 10 a guard phase (106) never fills it |
| capacity_floor | float | 84 (fixed) | data file | The lowest capacity whatever the share and max health: two combos (84), above a charged heavy (60). Stops a small `heal_share` or ratio making heals nearly free |
| max_health, heal_share | float | 100-180, 0.20-0.40 | F2 | Read as in F2, at the moment the meter fills or its capacity is shown |

**Output Range:** 0 to `meter_capacity`. The excess of a hit that crosses the cap is lost.
**Example:** meter at 100, a charged heavy lands (30 x 2 = 60): `min(100 + 60, 120)` = 120, and 40 is lost. At 180 max health the capacity is 216 and the same hit adds 60 to whatever is there. A first combo from empty: 10 → 22 → 42.

### F2. Heal amount

The heal_amount and start_deficit formulas are defined as:

`health' = min(health + heal_share x max_health, max_health)`

`start_deficit = max(1, min(start_deficit_share x heal_share x max_health, start_deficit_cap))`: the missing health a heal needs to start (rule 6)

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| health | float | above 0 up to `max_health` | calculated (`PlayerHealth.current`) | Health when the channel completes; a heal never starts at 0 (dead) or at full (rule 6) |
| max_health | float | 100-180 | calculated (collectible upgrades F2, registry `max_health_derived`) | The current derived max health, read at completion, never copied |
| heal_share | float | 0.20-0.40, starts at 0.30 | data file | The share of max health one heal restores. Below 0.20 a heal is too small to be worth a planted second; at 0.40 on 180 max health it is 72, just under two slams (80), and above about 0.44 it is more than two slams and the health pool stops mattering |

**Output Range:** the amount restored is `heal_share x max_health`: 30 at 100 max health, 54 at 180 (at the starting share), 20 to 72 across the share's range and the max health range; the cap makes the effective gain smaller near full health. The heal is 30 HP of a 40-damage slam at the start, 54 HP at full upgrades: its worth in boss hits grows by 1.8 times while the meter cost stays the same, which Open Question 6 asks the owner to watch.
**Example:** health 70 of 100: `min(70 + 30, 100)` = 100. Health 80 of 100: capped at 100, 10 of the 30 is unused (the meter is spent in full, rule 8). At 180 max and health 90: `min(90 + 54, 180)` = 144.

### F3. Hits per heal and the boss-cycle check

This is a tuning check, not a runtime calculation. The hits_per_heal, stagger_heals, cycle_heals and heal_ratio formulas are defined as:

`hits_per_heal = ceil(meter_capacity / mean_hit_damage)`
`start_meter = 0 if the banked heal is spent in the guard phase, else carry_in`: spending it needs the meter full and a safe 1.0 s in the guard phase
`meter_at_stagger = min(start_meter + guard_fill, meter_capacity)`: the meter the instant the guard breaks. Anything above `meter_capacity` is lost, so a meter carried in full and not spent wastes the whole guard phase's fill
`t_full = max(0, (meter_capacity - meter_at_stagger) / fill_rate)`: seconds into the stagger until the meter is full
`stagger_heals = max(0, floor((stagger_time - t_full + meter_capacity / fill_rate) / (channel_time + meter_capacity / fill_rate)))`: the heals that fit in the stagger. Channels are serial (nothing fills during one) and each needs a full refill. The term is 0 when the stagger is shorter than `t_full + channel_time`, so it never goes negative
`cycle_heals = guard_heals + stagger_heals`, where `guard_heals` is 1 when the meter is full at the start of the guard phase and the player spends it in a safe 1.0 s there, else 0. **Not a target** (AC-26)
`meter_at_end`: the meter carried into the next cycle (the fill left after the last heal, capped). A bar carried in below full cannot be spent in the guard phase, so the cycle count can alternate (Example 2)
`heal_nominal = heal_share x max_health`, `heal_effective` = the health actually gained after the cap, `heal_wasted = heal_nominal - heal_effective`; `heal_ratio = heal_effective_per_cycle / boss_damage_taken_per_cycle`: **logged, never a target** (AC-26). Health cannot exceed max, so in steady state the effective heal cannot exceed the damage taken and `heal_ratio` is at most about 1; above 1 only while recovering a deficit from earlier cycles. What the playtest reads is `heal_wasted`, how often a full bar sits unusable, and the heal's timing, not heals per cycle

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| meter_capacity | float | derived (F1): 120 at 100 max health, 216 at 180 | calculated | As in F1 |
| mean_hit_damage | float | 8-60 | calculated | The average damage of the hits the player uses; 14 for light combos (10, 12, 20 repeating: mean 14), 30 for uncharged heavies |
| carry_in | float | 0 to `meter_capacity` | calculated | The meter at the start of the guard phase. The meter keeps across fights (rule 4), so in steady play it starts at 0 (the carried heal was spent) or full (it was not) |
| guard_fill | float | 60-106 | calculated | Damage of the hits from a full guard to the breaking hit. **Two paths:** 106 for steady light combos (`guard-break.md` F2: 8 hits), and 60 for a charged heavy, which breaks the guard in one hit (30 x 2) and is the path the first boss playtest used |
| fill_rate | float | 0 upward | estimated | Fill per second while landing combos. About 49 for light combos: a 3-hit combo (42) takes about 0.86 s, from the clip durations in `seeker_animations.json` (attack1 chains at frame 5, about 0.18 s; attack2 at frame 4, about 0.16 s; attack3 plays out 0.52 s). **An estimate to be measured**, not a tested figure; the charged heavy's fill rate (0.55 s of hold plus the swing) is not estimated at all. At 0 `t_full` is undefined: the stagger gives 1 heal when the bar is already full and `stagger_time >= channel_time`, else 0 |
| stagger_time | float | 2.5 s default, 4.0 s boss | data file (`guard_config.tres`) | The window in which every hit lands at full damage. Hits do not extend it (`guard-break.md` rule 3) |
| t_full | float | 0 upward | calculated | Seconds into the stagger until the meter is full; 0 when the break already filled it |
| stagger_heals | int | 0 upward | calculated | Heals that fit in the stagger (formula above): 1 at the defaults on both break paths, up to 2 at the lowest capacities |
| cycle_heals | int | 0 upward | calculated | `guard_heals + stagger_heals`: 2 at best on the light path (Example 1), alternating 2 and 1 on the charged path (Example 2). **Not a target**: see Core Rules 12 and AC-26 |
| heal_nominal, heal_effective, heal_wasted | float | 0 upward | measured | `heal_share x max_health`, the health gained after the cap, and the difference. Logged separately (AC-26a) |
| heal_ratio | float | 0 to about 1 in steady state | measured | `heal_effective` per guard-break cycle, divided by the boss damage the Seeker took in it (n/a, logged as such, in a cycle with no damage taken). The number the owner reads in the playtest log |

**Output Range:** `hits_per_heal` is 2 (two charged heavies) to 15 (pogo hits at 8) at the defaults, and 2 (capacity 84, a charged heavy) to 38 (capacity 300, a pogo hit) across the capacity and damage ranges at 100 max health. `cycle_heals` has no target band, and neither has `heal_ratio`: they are numbers the playtests read (AC-26), not pass-fail checks **[Owner, 2026-11-22: determined by play]**.

**Example 1, boss, light-combo break, estimate.** The guard breaks on the 8th hit (attack1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 3, 1, 2), `guard_fill` = 106. Stagger 4.0 s.
- *Banked heal not spent (carry_in 120):* the meter is already full, the whole 106 is lost, `t_full` = 0 and `stagger_heals` = floor((4.0 + 2.45) / (1.0 + 2.45)) = 1: the player heals at once in the stagger and refills (3.0 s x 49 = about 147, capped at 120): **1 heal per cycle**, a full meter carried on.
- *Banked heal spent in the guard phase (carry_in 0):* the meter is 106 at the break and full 0.29 s into the stagger (14 more at 49 per second). `stagger_heals` = floor((4.0 - 0.29 + 2.45) / 3.45) = 1 (a second heal would need 3.45 s more than the 3.7 s left after the first channel), and `guard_heals` = 1 (it needs a safe 1.0 s in the guard phase): **2 heals per cycle** with a full meter carried on (the 2.7 s left after the stagger heal refills about 132, capped at 120).
- *HP, corrected in the third design review:* two heals are 60 HP nominal at 100 max and 108 at 180 max, but health cannot exceed max. Take one slam (40) in the cycle at 100 max: health 60, the guard-phase heal takes it to 90, and the stagger heal then has only 10 missing, below `start_deficit` (15), so it **cannot start** and the full bar waits (the meter is kept). The effective heal is about 30 to 40 against 40 taken, so `heal_ratio` is about 1.0, not the earlier 1.5 (which was nominal heals over damage). The playtest question is how often a full bar sits unusable and what `heal_wasted` is, and whether a boss that does not flinch is too easy with this much sustain (Open Question 10).

**Example 2, boss, charged-heavy break, estimate.** One charged heavy breaks the guard: `guard_fill` = 60 (the heavy needs 0.55 s of hold first). The meter is 60 at the break and full 1.2 s into the stagger (60 more at 49 per second). The player heals from 1.2 s to 2.2 s, then fills for the remaining 1.8 s: about 88, which is 0.73 of a bar, carried on (`t_full` = 1.22, `stagger_heals` = floor((4.0 - 1.22 + 2.45) / 3.45) = 1). **The next cycle** starts with 88 carried, which cannot be spent in the guard phase (it is not full): `meter_at_stagger` = min(88 + 60, 120) = 120 (28 lost), one stagger heal, and a full bar carried out. The cycle after that can spend the bar in the guard phase, so the path alternates 1 heal and 2 heals: **about 1.5 sustained**, not the earlier 1.7 (that figure ignored the cap). A player who does not heal in this stagger fills the meter full by 1.2 s and loses the other 2.8 s of fill (about 137). The route the owner found easiest to break the guard gives the smaller meter, and the first playtest's boss was beaten by it.

**Examples 1 and 2 are at 100 max health.** At 180 max health the capacity is 216: from a carry_in of 0 the guard phase (106) leaves `t_full` = (216 - 106) / 49 = 2.2 s, and the single stagger heal runs from 2.2 s to 3.2 s of the 4.0 s; with a full bar carried in, the guard phase's fill is lost and the stagger heals at once. Above about 210 max health (capacity above about 253) a stagger heal from empty no longer fits, which is a reason to bound max health (Open Question 18).

`HealingConfig.validate()` returns a `PackedStringArray` with the name of every value outside its range (`meter_cost_ratio`, `heal_share`, `channel_time`, `start_deficit_share`, `start_deficit_cap`, `press_buffer`, `heal_deceleration`). It runs when the Player is configured. The **caller** takes an injected `report: Callable` (production passes one that calls `push_error` naming the values), reports a non-empty result and clamps each value into its range **on a duplicate of the loaded resource**, never on the shared cached `.tres`, so a data typo never closes the game and a test asserts on the names the callable receives and on the clamped values without depending on the error stream. `@export_range` on each value gives the editor the same limits but never clamps a `.tres` at run time, so the clamp on load is the only runtime guard **[Rec, second design review; third and fourth: the report callable and the duplicate]**.

**Feedback loops:** one positive loop, intended: a heal keeps the player alive longer, so they land more hits and earn more heals. It has two real dampers: a heal restores at most the health the player is missing (so effective healing cannot exceed the damage taken), and a boss's health is a finite stock. The cap on the meter is **not** a damper at normal fill rates (a bar every 2.5 s of hitting at about 49 fill per second), and the heal share sets what one heal gives, not the loop gain. Deriving the capacity from the heal (F1) keeps the loop gain the same at every max health: a kill repays about the same share of the damage it cost, instead of 1.8 times more at 180 max health than at 100. The channel's risk is not a damper against this boss (Core Rules 12), and the loop is not damped against farming (Edge Cases). **Seam:** collectible upgrades F3 raises sword health damage by up to x1.3; this system reads the damage **before** that multiplier, so edge steps do not make a swing fill faster (Dependencies), although kills then take fewer swings, so fill per kill falls by up to about 23%.

## Edge Cases

`systems-designer` not consulted for this section: Lean mode (the second review's full-mode specialists then checked it). Every case below is a **[Rec]** unless tagged otherwise, and the system is not built, so none is "handled in the code".

**Starting and ending a heal**
- **If `heal` is pressed with the meter full and health full**: nothing starts, nothing is spent, and the health refusal cue plays (missing health is below `start_deficit`). The meter stays full for the next fight.
- **If `heal` is pressed with the meter below capacity**: nothing starts and the meter-not-full refusal cue plays. A press that fails on the meter or the health is never buffered: a press 0.1 s before the meter fills does not start a heal later.
- **If `heal` is pressed with missing health below `start_deficit`** (15 at the defaults, so health 86 to 100 of 100): nothing starts, nothing is spent and the health refusal cue plays. A heal at 85 of 100 or lower starts.
- **If `heal` is pressed while the Seeker is in `ATTACK`, `HURT` or `AIR`** (a state that ends within `press_buffer`, 0.12 s; a press during the landing or standing-up animation starts the heal at once, rule 6): the press is remembered for `press_buffer`; if every condition of rule 6 holds in that window and the input is still held, the heal starts on that tick (rule 14). A press that is released before the state ends starts nothing.
- **If `heal` was already held for longer than `press_buffer` when the conditions began to hold** (for example held through an attack or the end of hurt lock): it does not start a heal. The player must release and press again, so holding the button through a fight never starts a 1.0 s plant by accident.
- **If `heal` is pressed crouched, on a ladder, grappling, on a wall, dashing, on a ledge or dead**: nothing starts (rule 6: the state is not `GROUND`), nothing is buffered and no cue plays in a state where the reason is already visible (movement-state refusals are silent, UI Requirements).
- **If `heal` is pressed during a door transition or a rest**: the controller has locked input (`save-points-and-save-load.md`, Resting), so the press never reaches the heal logic: nothing starts and **no cue plays**.
- **If the Seeker is inside its invulnerability window (after a landed hit) and hurt lock is over**: a heal may start. The 1.0 s window leaves 0.65 s of protection after the 0.35 s hurt lock, so the first 0.65 s of such a channel is safe; this is accepted (owner, 2026-11-23; rule 10, AC-17b) and is part of what Open Question 10's playtest reads, not a bug.
- **If `interact` is pressed in a door or rest area while channelling**: the door or rest takes over and the channel is cancelled with the meter kept (rule 9). Movement cannot carry the Seeker through an exit during a channel because the Seeker is planted.

**During the channel**
- **If a hit lands on the same physics tick the timer reaches `channel_time`**: the hit is resolved first, the channel is cancelled and no heal happens. The mechanism is rule 15 (the Seeker's `process_physics_priority` is above every `take_hit` caller, the boss's `Shockwave` included), so tests are deterministic and do not depend on scene-tree order.
- **If `heal` is released on the same tick the timer reaches `channel_time`**: the heal completes (rule 9). A jump, dash, grapple or attack press on that tick cancels instead and the heal is lost (rule 15, step b): the asymmetry is on purpose.
- **If `heal` is released at any time before the completion tick** (or a toggle's second press): the Seeker is in `GROUND` that tick with no cost, the meter is kept and the time channelled is lost. There is no recovery (rule 9).
- **If a jump, dash, grapple or attack is pressed during `CHANNEL`**: the channel ends and the action starts on the same tick (an attack arms Combat's tap/hold resolution), with no recovery. The jump buffer is not cleared on a jump cancel, so the jump that caused it is not eaten and does not fire twice (rule 9).
- **If the Seeker slides off an edge during the planting skid**: the channel is cancelled (involuntary, no recovery) and the Seeker is in AIR.
- **If a hit arrives in the invulnerability window** (`take_hit` returns false): the channel is not cancelled, since no damage was dealt (rule 9).
- **If max health changes during the channel** (a collectible upgrade completes a health step, which also sets health to the new max): F2 reads max health at completion, so the heal uses the new value. The capacity is recomputed and the meter keeps its fraction (F1), so a full bar stays full.
- **If health is already at max when the channel completes** (a health step refilled it mid-channel): the heal is not applied and the meter is **kept**; nothing is wasted. The channel ends without a cue of success.
- **If the Seeker dies during the channel**: the channel is cancelled, the meter is zeroed on `revive` (rule 5).
- **If the game is paused or the time scale drops** (pause stops the pausable Seeker; hit-stop and slow-motion change how much game time a physics tick carries, because `Engine.time_scale` scales `delta`: an unverified claim, the engine reference has no scripting page, checked in the first story): the channel timer accumulates the physics `delta` (rule 7), so a pause freezes it and hit-stop stretches it in real time but not in game time; it neither completes nor cancels while paused, and a tick count holds only at time scale 1.0. Hit-stop today fires on a foe's death (`enemy_base.gd`), which can happen during the Seeker's own channel, so the first story checks the count under it; the existing `_hit_stop` resets `time_scale` after an `await`, so a foe freed before the timer fires could leave the scale stuck and silently stretch every channel (to verify; the fix is to restore the scale from a static or autoload counter, not from a foe that may have been freed, and to count overlapping hit-stops so one does not restore early). The Player must stay `PROCESS_MODE_PAUSABLE`. Unit tests drive `delta` directly and never touch `Engine.time_scale`.
- **If a foe the Seeker cannot see lands a hit**: it cancels like any hit; there is no "safe" cancel.
- **If `heal_mode` is `toggle`**: a press starts the channel and it runs without the input held; a second press is a release (rule 9). A press that fails to start does not arm the toggle, a press consumed by the pause menu is not a heal press, and a second press on the completion tick completes the heal as a release does.

**Filling**
- **If one swing hits two foes**: each foe's hit adds its own `damage` (Combat reports one `hit_landed` per target per swing).
- **If the killing blow lands**: its damage counts, once.
- **If a pogo (down attack) hits a foe**: it adds 8 per hit, the lowest fill of any attack. A pogo on a hazard, a breakable or a pane adds nothing (rule 3).
- **If the meter is full and more hits land**: the excess is lost and no cue plays.
- **If the Seeker dies in the same tick it lands a hit**: the meter is zeroed on `revive` regardless, so the fill does not survive.
- **If damage is 0 or negative** (a bad attack entry): it adds nothing (F1's `max(damage, 0)`); the meter never falls from a hit.
- **If `HealingConfig.validate()` finds a value out of range**: it is reported with `push_error` naming the value and clamped into its range; no heal runs with a bad number and the game keeps running.

**Boss and balance**
- **If the player heals during a guard-break stagger** (4.0 s for the boss): allowed, and it costs 1.0 s of punish damage on the boss's health. Because the planned stages end on a guard-break count, not on the boss's health, that damage is not what progress needs until the last stage, so the cost is close to nothing (Core Rules 12). Healing inside a stagger is a choice, not an exploit; whether it is too cheap is what the heal-ratio log reads (F3, AC-26).
- **If the Seeker dies to the boss and the fight resets**: the meter is 0 on revive (rule 5); the player starts the next attempt with no heal.
- **If a boss stage change happens mid-channel**: the stage change is a boss event and cancels nothing here; a hit that lands as part of it cancels as usual. What a stage change does for the player's heal is an open question.
- **Farming (known loop, accepted for now):** a foe that is rebuilt on room re-entry (rooms and transitions rule 12) can be killed again and again to fill the meter, so a player can top up health between rests. Hollow Knight has the same loop. Level design already keeps every enemy's leash bound away from a rest area (save points rule 3a), so a rest itself is never farmed. On paper (third design review's economy lens) looping one rebuilt grunt, skitter or brute with no damage taken yields about 6 to 12 bars of fill a minute. The health gained is bounded by the player's missing HP (recovering from empty takes about 3.3 heals, roughly 30 to 60 s of looping), so this is a rest-point substitute, not unbounded income. Whether to cap it (a diminishing fill on the same room's foes) is an Open Question with a measurable trigger (Open Question 3).

**Implementation notes (for the first story; not design rules).** (1) *Tick order (rule 15):* the Seeker's `process_physics_priority` is set in `Player._ready` to 10, above every node that calls `Player.take_hit` (a lower value runs first and the default is 0): the `EnemyBase` melee (`_state_attack`), the boss's melee and the boss's `Shockwave` `Area2D` (an inner class added to the arena at run time that polls `get_overlapping_bodies()` in its own `_physics_process`). The rule is stated by **caller**, not by class, and a future hazard that calls `take_hit` must stay at priority 0 or below; a grep of `take_hit` in `src/` is the audit (AC-18d). Hits are delivered by polling inside the hitter's own tick, not by signal, so a hit has already landed when the Seeker's heal logic runs; a completion step that re-tests after the foes cannot see a hitter that acts later, so only the priority works. The priority is global to the Seeker: it also moves `health.tick`, `PlayerCombat.update` and the boss arena's `_physics_process` after the hitters, so the invulnerability-end and hurt-lock-end boundary cases in `player-controller.md` are re-derived with numbers when it lands (a hitter now sees the previous tick's invulnerability decrement, and the Seeker's own hits land after the foes have acted on that tick). A channel is cancelled in `_set_state`'s exit path, not in `take_hit`, and the meter reset on `revive` belongs there too. Unit tests that call `_physics_process` cannot prove ordering: AC-18 steps real frames. (2) *Cancel dispatch (rule 9):* `_state_heal` calls `_set_state(GROUND)` and then `_state_ground(delta)` on the same tick; the jump buffer, written every tick before the state dispatch (`player.gd` about lines 229-231), is zeroed on entry to `HEAL` and on every exit but a jump cancel. (3) *Test seam:* the per-tick heal logic is a pure `HealChannel` step (`held`, `just_pressed`, `cancel_press` and `delta` in; state and events out), so the input-driven ACs are unit tests; a hand-called `_physics_process` cannot drive the real `Input`. (4) *Signals (the contract the HUD, Hit feedback and Audio listen to):* `heal_started`, `heal_completed(effective: float)`, `heal_cancelled(reason: StringName)`, `meter_full` and `heal_refused(reason: StringName)`, with reason `&"meter"` or `&"health"` (a movement-state refusal emits nothing). (5) *Animation:* `heal_end` is gated by a flag reset in `_set_state`, like `_landing` and `_standing_up`, because `GROUND` replays `idle` every tick; a missing clip is a hard error, so the clips and sheets ship before the state code. (6) *Groups:* `foe` is a `const GROUP_FOE` in `PlayerCombat`, and the fill handler guards with `is_instance_valid(target)` for the killing blow.

## Visual/Audio Requirements

`art-director` lens applied in-session (the heal's cues are what the player reads to play, so Lean mode runs it) against `design/art/art-bible.md` sections 1-4. `audio-director` and `sound-designer` not consulted: Lean mode, and no audio direction or asset exists yet (Audio is index #25, Not Started). Review manually before production.

**Art bible principles that apply.** *Principle 3, Combat owns the light:* the sword arc and the guard-break spark are the brightest events on screen, so no heal effect may out-shine them. *Principle 2, the greyscale order:* the Seeker is the lightest thing in the room, and the heal glow raises the Seeker, never anything around it. *Principle 1, crisp means contact:* effects on the Seeker are pixel art at 4x with nearest filtering. *Section 4, what colour means:* warm and pale is "you" (Bone Pale), steady Kiln Amber is "safe" (the rest pool), cool ice-white is "an enemy attack to dodge", and the four key colours (orange, chalk-yellow, copper-green, violet) belong to the abilities. The heal uses none of the last three.

| Event | Visual | Audio (direction only) | Limits |
|-------|--------|------------------------|--------|
| A hit fills the meter | On the meter only: it brightens at the new fill edge and settles in about 0.15 s. Nothing in the world | None; the hit's own sound carries the moment **[Rec]** | Combat owns the light: no spark or glow in the room for a fill |
| The meter becomes full (ready) | One soft pulse on the Seeker: its pale shade warms a half-step and eases back, as in the rest moment (art bible, Resting), plus the meter's full state (**banked**, shown on the HUD). **While a heal is usable** (meter full and missing health at least `start_deficit`) the Seeker carries one small, steady, non-colour marker (a rim or a silhouette notch in Bone Pale, no animation), so a player can see "a heal is available" mid-fight without looking at the HUD and without relying on colour. It goes out when the meter is spent or health rises above the threshold, and it needs at least 3:1 contrast against Void Black and the lightest biome background and at least 2 source pixels of thickness **[Rec]**; final shape and size are the UX spec's (Open Question 20). A banked bar with health too high to heal shows the HUD state only | A short rising tone with a timbre of its own, not a variant of the refusal tick. It must be audible over boss music: a requirement for the sound designer | Pulse eased over at least 0.5 s, once per 0-to-full crossing (not on overflow, a revive reset or a load); never a flicker. (c) None | Never red. **Rate limit: at most one refusal cue per 0.5 s**, so mashing the input gives at most 2 cue flashes a second, inside the registry `flash_limit` of 3 with room for the other heal lights. **With Flash reduction On the refusal cue is not a luminance change at all** (a small positional nudge of the meter or a bracket shift, no brightness change), and the cap of 3 per second holds with Flash reduction Off, which is the default (pause rule 14). The glow is steady, nothing flickers. **Requirement, not a recipe:** the glow is capped at Bone Pale (luma 0.64) whatever the sprite shows under it; it is not multiplied by the heavy-ready flash's `modulate` and does not follow the invulnerability blink (the blink owns `self_modulate.a`); and it cannot add above the cap (no additive blend over the lightest sprite). How it is drawn is the first story's call, verified by AC-27: a shader material on the Seeker's own sprite with a `heal_t` uniform that mixes the colour toward the capped target and keeps alpha is the likely route; a child overlay sprite cannot read the parent sprite's pixels, so a `mix(base, ...)` there does not work. The project has no Environment glow, and the idle aura is gated on `GROUND`, so it is hidden in `HEAL` |

| Cancelled (release, a hit, a movement press, a slide off an edge) | The glow fades out over at least 0.3 s with no flash while control returns at once; a hit's own hurt effect plays over it | A short cut-off | Not a flash: a fall of 10% luminance or more counts toward the flash limit, so the fade is never faster than the limit allows. Tapping start and cancel five times in a second must still stay at or under 3 luminance events (AC-27) |

**Flash limit.** The pulse, the bloom, the refusal cues, the cancel fade and the glow count toward registry `flash_limit` (3 per second, WCAG 2.3.1) **together**, not one by one; with Flash reduction On they must read as steady glows and the refusal cue becomes a positional nudge with no luminance change (so AC-27 expects 0 luminance events then); the combined cap of 3 per second also holds with Flash reduction Off, the default. Every heal light on the Seeker is drawn through one heal-light channel (the channel row above); the invulnerability blink owns `self_modulate.a` and the heavy-ready flash owns `modulate`. AC-27 states that HDR output is off and records the renderer setting with the capture, because with HDR on a value above 1.0 can display brighter than SDR white and defeat the caps. AC-27 measures the combined result on a retained capture. With the atmosphere set to Reduced (parallax rule 14) the heal still reads through the Seeker's pose, the persistent full marker and the meter, not through bloom.

**Audio constraint, event contract and owner (fourth design review).** One negative constraint is kept, **provisional until Bosses defines a telegraph sound** (`bosses.md` has no audio text today): the channel swell must not share the shape of a boss attack charge-up (its rise is in loudness, not a pitch sweep, and it stays in a low, narrow band), so a player channelling can still hear a wind-up. **Event contract (gameplay behaviour, fixed here):** the ready tone fires only on a 0-to-full crossing caused by a landed hit, never on overflow, a revive reset, a load, a room entry or a rest; the swell starts on `heal_started` and stops within one tick of every exit (`heal_completed`, `heal_cancelled`), driven by the channel's progress so a pause freezes it; and every heal audio cue has a visual twin and none is the only carrier of information (the 0.5 s pip has the visible tick above). Owed to the Audio GDD (index #25, owner the audio-director, Open Question 19): mix priority and ducking against boss telegraphs, anti-fatigue for the ready tone (it can fire every 10 s or so in a boss fight), a cancel sound per exit, loudness targets and the audio acceptance criteria beyond AC-39's event wiring.

**Animation.** `seeker_animations.json` has no heal clip today (clips: idle, run, jump, dash, crouch, attack1-3, heavy, hurt, death and others), and its schema takes a `sheet`, `from`, `to` and `loop` per clip. New clips: `heal_loop` (a planted pose, `loop` true, for the channel) and `heal_end` (not looping, for the completion; it is cosmetic, since control returns on the completion tick and any input interrupts it; it is gated by a flag reset in `_set_state`, like `_landing`, because `GROUND` replays `idle` every tick; its end needs its own case in the player's `_on_animation_finished`, since a looping clip never finishes). Frame counts are the animator's and must not be tied to `channel_time`. The heavy's charge hold is the nearest existing pose to match for weight.

**HUD colours** (for the HUD GDD): the meter and its full state must not use a key colour or rely on red against green (art bible, UI palette), and the full state needs a shape or brightness cue, not a hue change alone (colourblind rule).

## Game Feel

**Feel reference.** Hollow Knight's Focus: a held, planted commitment that a hit breaks. The difference here is that the meter is kept on a cancel, so a mistake costs time and damage, not the work of earning the meter.

| Target | Value | Why |
|--------|-------|-----|
| Press to `CHANNEL` | the same physics tick (no start-up) | A heal the player has chosen must start when pressed; the wait is the channel, not input lag |
| First visible response | within 1 tick (the pose and the glow start) | The player sees the commitment begin |
| Cancel to control | By a jump, dash or grapple press: 0 s, the action starts on the same tick. By an attack press: 0 s, Combat's tap/hold resolution is armed on the same tick. By letting go (or a toggle's second press): 0 s. After a hit: the controller's hurt lock as usual | A movement press must always be the free way out (pillar 1), and a slip must cost nothing |
| Completion to control | 0 s (control on the completion tick) | The heal is the reward; no extra lock after it |
| Refusal cue | within 1 tick of the press | A press that did nothing must be seen to do nothing |
| Ready cue | the tick the meter reaches capacity | The player learns the heal is available without looking away from the fight |
| Press buffer | a press up to `press_buffer` (0.12 s) before the Seeker can heal starts the heal on the first tick it can, if still held | After a hit or a swing the first instinct is to press heal; losing that press silently feels like a bug |

**Weight.** The channel is a planted 1.0 s with no armour. A heal started right after a boss strike ends 0.6 to 0.9 s before the next wind-up begins (gaps of 2.4 to 3.0 s, Core Rules 12), so at the current timings the only risk is a hit that lands (it loses the second spent) and a shockwave already in flight; the boss patterns that punish a planted Seeker are owed (Open Question 12), and in a stagger (4.0 s) a heal is nearly free. **Hit-stop and screen shake:** a heal adds none. These targets are checked in the owner's playtest, not by an automated test (AC-26); "feels earned" is not a threshold.

## UI Requirements

## Acceptance Criteria

The `qa-lead` lens was applied in-session in the first review and by a specialist in the second and fourth. **Defaults** are the starting values at player `max_health` 100: `meter_capacity` 120 (derived, 4.0 x 30), `heal_share` 0.30, `channel_time` 1.0 s, `start_deficit` 15, `press_buffer` 0.12 s and `run_speed` 140 (read from `player_config.tres`, not typed into the test). The ACs run against a pinned fixture (`tests/fixtures/healing_config_test.tres` **[Rec]**) holding these values, so a balance pass does not break them. Tags: **[Logic]** automated unit test, **[Integration]** automated integration test with the real nodes, **[UI]** retained screenshot or capture in `production/qa/evidence/`, **[Playtest]** owner playtest. A physics tick is 1/60 s; tick 0 is the press tick (timer 0), the timer after tick N is N x `delta`, and every timing is counted in ticks. The input-driven Logic tests drive the pure `HealChannel` step (Dependencies, Implementation notes) with an explicit `held`, `just_pressed` and `delta` per tick; integration tests step the real physics server (a hitter's overlap is established at least one step earlier, because `get_overlapping_*` report the last step; a pause test uses a real `get_tree().paused`); none touches `Engine.time_scale`. A test that reads `heal` calls `PlayerInputBindings.register()` first. Every "no cue" assertion is paired with a positive control (the same press in a state that does emit), so it cannot pass vacuously. AC-9a, AC-9d, AC-19 and the door variant of AC-20 run today. **AC-9b, AC-9c, AC-20c and AC-32b are closed by the stories that build the Continue and new-game, rest and room-change flows (Save points, Rooms and transitions), not by Healing's story**, and a story may not close on a stub that passes vacuously. **AC-25, AC-27b to 27e and AC-31 are not closable until the HUD GDD, the heal clip and a flash-analysis tool (owner: tools-programmer, `tools/`) exist.**

**The meter**
- **AC-1 Fill [Logic].** GIVEN the meter is 0, WHEN attack1, attack2 and attack3 land on a foe in turn (damage 10, 12, 20), THEN the meter reads 10, 22 and 42. **AC-1b:** a pogo (down attack, damage 8) on a foe adds 8.
- **AC-2 Cap [Logic].** GIVEN the meter is 100, WHEN a charged heavy lands (damage 60), THEN the meter is 120 (not 160); WHEN a further hit lands, THEN it stays 120 and `meter_full` was emitted exactly once across both hits.
- **AC-3 Guarded hits fill in full [Integration].** GIVEN the boss is GUARDED and the meter is 0, WHEN attack1 lands, THEN the meter is 10 and the boss's health fell by 2.0 (damage x 0.2): the fill ignores the guard multiplier.
- **AC-4 Only foes feed it [Integration].** One test per target. GIVEN the meter is 30, WHEN the down attack hits (a) a hazard, (b) a breakable, (c) a pane, (d) a `HitTarget`, THEN the meter stays 30. **AC-4b:** WHEN it hits an `EnemyBase`, a boss hurtbox or the `TrainingDummy` (all in group `foe`), THEN the meter rises by the hit's damage.
- **AC-5 Killing blow [Integration].** GIVEN a foe has 5 health and the meter is 0, WHEN attack1 (damage 10) kills it, THEN the meter is 10, added once.
- **AC-6 One swing, two foes [Integration].** GIVEN two foes overlap one attack1 swing and the meter is 0, WHEN the swing lands, THEN the meter is 20.
- **AC-7 Bad damage [Logic].** GIVEN the meter is 50, WHEN a hit reports damage 0, and then another reports -5, THEN the meter stays 50 after each.
- **AC-8 No decay, fills at full health [Integration].** GIVEN health is 100 of 100 and the meter is 60, WHEN 3600 physics ticks pass with no hit, THEN the meter is 60; **AC-8b:** WHEN a hit then lands, THEN the meter rises.
- **AC-9 Resets [Logic; 9a and 9d run today, 9b and 9c are not closable until the flows exist].** GIVEN the meter is 80: **AC-9a** a death and `revive` leave 0; **AC-9b** a Continue and a new game each leave 0; **AC-9c** a rest and a room change each leave 80; **AC-9d** the meter is not part of the saved record (World state rule 1).

**Starting a heal**
- **AC-10 Not ready [Logic].** One test per case, each with the meter full unless stated. WHEN `heal` is pressed: **10a** meter 119: no `HEAL`, nothing spent, the meter-not-full refusal cue; **10b** health 86 of 100 (missing 14, below `start_deficit` 15): no `HEAL`, nothing spent, the health refusal cue; **10c** health 85 of 100: `HEAL` starts (the boundary); **10d** one test for each state `AIR`, `CROUCH`, `ATTACK`, `HURT`, `CLIMB`, `WALL_SLIDE`, `DASH`, `GRAPPLE`, `LEDGE` and `DEAD`: no `HEAL`, nothing spent and **no cue** (movement-state refusals are silent); for `AIR`, `ATTACK` and `HURT`, which rule 14 buffers, the state is held for at least 8 ticks after the press (or the input is released before it ends) so the buffer expires; **10e** (priority, rule 6): meter 50 with health 100 gives the meter cue only; meter 50 in `AIR` gives no cue; meter 120 with health 100 in `AIR` gives no cue. **10f** (the cap, max health 180): health 161 of 180 (missing 19) is refused, health 160 (missing 20, `start_deficit_cap`) starts. **10g** (the floor): with `start_deficit_share` 0, health 100 of 100 is refused and 99 starts. A press during a door transition or a rest is AC-32.
- **AC-11 Fresh press and buffer [Logic].** **11a** GIVEN `heal` was held for 30 ticks through an attack and the meter is full, WHEN the attack ends, THEN no heal starts; WHEN the input is released and pressed again, THEN `HEAL` starts. **11b** GIVEN `heal` is pressed 7 ticks (0.117 s) before an attack's lock ends and is still held, THEN the heal starts on the first tick the Seeker is `GROUND`; if it is released first, nothing starts; if it was pressed 8 or more ticks (0.133 s) before, nothing starts (the buffer boundary is between 7 and 8 ticks). **11c** GIVEN the meter reaches capacity 3 ticks after a press, THEN no heal starts (a press that fails on the meter is never buffered).

**The channel**
- **AC-12 Planted [Integration].** GIVEN the Seeker runs at `run_speed` with a full meter and health 70 of 100, WHEN `heal` is pressed, THEN horizontal speed is 0 by tick 5, the total displacement from the press is at most 6.5 px, and from then until the channel ends the position does not change (the skid stops 140 px/s on a 2000 px/s2 deceleration in about 4.2 ticks and about 5 px; the bound allows the order of movement and deceleration within a tick).
- **AC-13 Completion [Logic].** GIVEN the meter is 120: **13a** health 70 of 100, WHEN `heal` is held for the channel, THEN health is 100, the meter is 0 and the Seeker is back in GROUND on that tick; **13b** health 50: 80; **13c** max health 180, the meter 216 and health 90: 144, meter 0; **13d** health 85 of 100: 100, meter 0 (15 of the 30 is unused).
- **AC-14 Not before time [Logic].** GIVEN a channel started on tick 0, THEN after tick 59 (0.983 s) the meter is still 120 and health is unchanged, and the heal is applied on tick 60 exactly.
- **AC-15 Letting go [Logic].** **15a (hold mode)** GIVEN a channel, WHEN `heal` is released on any tick from 1 to 59, THEN the Seeker is in GROUND on that tick, health is unchanged, the meter is 120 and no recovery applies. **15b (completion tick)** WHEN `heal` is released on tick 60, THEN the heal completes (health + 30, meter 0). **15c (toggle mode)** WHEN a second press cancels at tick 30, THEN the Seeker is in GROUND that tick; a second press on tick 60 completes the heal. **15d (time lost)** GIVEN a cancel at tick 30 and a new fresh press after it, THEN the heal completes 60 ticks after the new press, not 30.
- **AC-16 Movement cancels [Integration].** One test per action. GIVEN a channel at tick 30, WHEN **16a** jump, **16b** dash or **16c** grapple (owned) is pressed, THEN the channel ends that tick, the action starts that tick, the meter is 120 and health is unchanged. **16d** WHEN attack is pressed, THEN the channel ends that tick, the state is GROUND and Combat has a pending attack (its tap/hold resolution is armed: a tap swings on release, a hold becomes the heavy, exactly as in GROUND).
- **AC-17 A hit cancels, no protection [Integration].** **17a** GIVEN a channel at tick 30 and a foe attack of 15 damage, WHEN it lands, THEN health falls by the full 15, the meter stays 120, no heal occurs and the Seeker is in HURT. **17b** GIVEN a full meter, a first hit of 15 damage or more on absolute tick 0, a fresh `heal` press on tick 22 (past the 21-tick hurt lock) and a second hit at channel tick 30 (absolute tick 52, inside the 60-tick invulnerability window), THEN `take_hit` returns false, the state is still `HEAL`, the meter is 120, health is unchanged and the timer keeps running. **17c:** the same with the second hit at channel tick 40 (absolute tick 62, past the window): `take_hit` returns true, the Seeker is in HURT and the meter is kept.
- **AC-18 Hit and completion on one tick [Integration, real SceneTree].** GIVEN a channel and a hitter whose overlap with the Seeker was established at least one physics step earlier, WHEN the hitter's `take_hit` runs on the completing tick (tick 60, the step whose timer advance would reach 1.0 s), THEN the hit lands first, the channel is cancelled, no heal occurs, the meter is 120 and the Seeker is in HURT (Core Rules 15).
