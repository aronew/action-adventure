## Summary

The grid map is a full-screen map on its own button that draws the world as boxes on the world grid, one cell per screen, filling in screen by screen as the Seeker sees it; a region map found in each region outlines that region's rooms not yet entered. It shows where the Seeker is, the save niches it has seen with the respawn niche highlighted, and a marker on every exit to a place not yet seen, but never items or secrets. It exists so the player can hold a world of large rooms and loops in view, and it keeps no state of its own: what has been revealed lives in World state.

> **Quick reference** — Layer: `Presentation` · Priority: `MVP` · Key deps: `Rooms and transitions, World state, Level design`

## Overview

The grid map is the Seeker's picture of the world it has walked. Pressing the map button opens a full screen that freezes play and draws each region as boxes on the world grid: one box per room, sized by its screens, with lines for its exits and marks for its doors (level design rule 23, rooms and transitions rules 2 and 15). A room is drawn screen by screen as the Seeker sees it, so a large room appears as it is crossed and the map fills in from the first step of a new game with nothing to buy and nothing to remember to do (the brief: "a grid map that fills in"). Each region also hides a **region map** somewhere in its rooms, a found object, never sold; picking it up draws that region's unseen screens as faint outlines, so a curious player is rewarded with the shape of what is left without being shown what is in it. An exit whose far side has not been seen shows as a marker, which is how the map points at unfinished business; a secret room or hidden way shows nothing until found. The map shows where the Seeker is (its room highlighted and a mark at its position) and every save niche on a screen it has seen, with the niche it last rested at highlighted, so the player always knows where a death sends them. It never marks items, keys, secrets' contents or bosses (level design rule 23). The map keeps no state of its own: the screens seen and the region maps found are World state's (a new revealed-cells entry; a pickup's `collected` flag), and the boxes, lines and niches come from the room data and the room graph Rooms and transitions derives. Without it, a world of large rooms and loops would be held only in the player's head, the unexplored-exit markers level design relies on would have nowhere to show, and the brief's last unticked feature would stay unticked. **[Owner, 2026-11-20: fills in as the world is seen, Ori-style, screen by screen, with a found region map that outlines the rest; its own button, full screen; the Seeker's room and position shown; save niches marked once seen; secrets nothing until found]**

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

Tags: **[Owner]** = your decision; **[Rec]** = recommendation, change freely. `game-designer`, `ux-designer`, `ui-programmer`, `art-director` and `technical-artist` not consulted: Lean mode. Review manually before production.

**What the map draws**

1. **Map cells.** The map draws the world grid (rooms and transitions rule 2): one **map cell** per world-grid cell, that is per 480 x 270 screen, at a fixed scale (Formulas F1). A room is the block of cells it covers; a region is the rooms that name it. All regions share the one grid, so the map is one picture of the whole world, not a page per region. **[Rec]**
2. **Revealed screens.** A cell of a room is **revealed** on the first frame that the Seeker's centre is inside it, or that at least `reveal_cover` of the cell lies inside the camera's view (Formulas F2). A revealed cell stays revealed. World state keeps the revealed cells as a new entry: for each room id, the set of room-local cells (column, row from the room's top-left) revealed so far. Room-local, so a room moved on the world grid keeps them (rooms and transitions rule 3); a cell outside a resized room is kept and not drawn (world state rule 10). It is raised the moment it happens and only grows (world state rules 3, 5). Entering a room reveals at least the cell the Seeker arrives in, so a room visited always has a revealed cell. A **secret cell** (rule 7) is revealed only by the Seeker's centre, never by camera cover, so looking at a secret section from outside it does not put it on the map. This field is a change owed by World state (Dependencies). **[Owner, 2026-11-20: screen by screen; Rec: the reveal test; Rec, review 2026-11-20: secret cells by the centre only]**
3. **Drawing a room.** A revealed cell is drawn filled in its region's map colour. A room's border is drawn along every edge of a revealed cell that lies on the room's bounds; no line is drawn between two revealed cells of the same room, so a room reads as one shape as it is uncovered. An unrevealed cell of an entered room is drawn as a cell of an unentered room is (rule 4). **[Rec]**
4. **The region map.** Each region holds one **region map**: a pickup placed by level design, with an object id (rooms and transitions rule 3) and the `collected` flag every non-key pickup has (world state rule 2). It is a found object, never sold. Once it is collected, the bounds of every room of its region, except a secret room (rule 7), are drawn as a faint outline wherever no revealed cell's border is drawn: the room's whole rectangle, no fill, no exits and no niches. A secret section of a larger room is outlined with its room like any other cells, so the outline is a plain rectangle with no notch where the section lies and the map does not say that anything is secret there; an unrevealed cell, secret or not, is otherwise drawn as nothing. Collecting it reveals nothing (rule 2), adds nothing to the record but its flag, and shows the pickup cue (Visual/Audio). Before it is collected, an unentered room is not drawn at all. **[Owner, 2026-11-20: Ori-style, a found region map outlines the rest; Owner, review 2026-11-20: the room's full rectangle, no notch]**
5. **Exits.** An opening may be any number of tiles wide and may cross a screen's edge (rooms and transitions rule 4), so each exit has a point to draw at, its **anchor**: for an open edge, the midpoint of its opening's span on the room's bounds; for a door, the centre of its area. Along the border, a point on a cell edge belongs to the cell right of or below it (Edge Cases). An exit has two **sides**, and a side is **seen** when any of its **exit cells** is revealed. For an open edge, a side's exit cells are the cells on that side of the border that the opening's span touches: one cell, or several when the opening crosses a screen's edge, so crossing the exit anywhere along its opening sees the side the Seeker arrives on. The anchor decides only where the line is drawn. For a door, a side's exit cells are the cell holding the door's anchor and the cell holding the Seeker's arrival point in front of it (rooms and transitions rule 7), whichever is revealed, so a door whose area straddles a cell edge is seen from either. An open edge is drawn as a short line across the room's border at its anchor once either side is seen (an open edge is symmetric, rule 6). A door is drawn as a door mark at its anchor once its own side is seen. A door pair is not joined by a line: doors need not line up on the grid, and a line between them would cross other rooms. An opening that is solid because the region beyond is not built is not an exit (rooms and transitions Edge Cases) and is not drawn. **[Rec; level design rule 23: exits as lines; review 2026-11-20: sides seen by any cell of the opening]**
6. **Unexplored markers.** A drawn exit carries a marker while its far side is not seen: for an open edge, while exactly one of its two sides is seen (it is drawn once either is, rule 5); for a door, while its pair's side is not seen. The marker goes on the frame that side is seen. A gate across an exit does not hide the exit or its marker: an exit the Seeker cannot yet cross is unfinished business, which is what the marker is for (level design rules 8 and 23). **[Rec; level design rule 23]**
7. **Secrets are never given away.** A **secret cell** is a cell the room data marks secret: every cell of a secret room (a room whose job is secret, level design rule 18) and every cell of a secret section of a larger room (a section whose job is secret, the same rule). A secret cell is revealed only by the Seeker's centre, never by camera cover (rule 2). A region map never outlines a secret room (rule 4); a secret section of a larger room is outlined only as part of its room's rectangle. A **hidden exit** is an exit the room data marks hidden: every exit behind a breakable wall or other hidden way, and every exit into a secret cell from a cell that is not secret. The cells of one side of an opening are all secret or all not; the room check rejects a mix. The mark is on one side only, the exit's **near side**: the room the exit is hidden from, which holds the hiding object, or, with no hiding object, the room whose side is not secret. The other side is its **far side**. The mark names the **hiding object**, an object id in the near-side room (a breakable wall, a door, a shortcut or a secret), or names none when nothing physical hides the exit (an open edge into a secret room). A hidden exit is neither drawn nor marked until its hiding object's `broken`, `open` or `found` is raised, or its far side is seen (rule 5); an exit with no hiding object is drawn only when its far side is seen. Once drawn it is an ordinary exit (rules 5, 6): it carries a marker while exactly one of its sides is seen, so a passage found from the secret side points back at the unvisited room beyond it. **[Owner, 2026-11-20: nothing until found; review 2026-11-20: secret cells marked in the data, a marker once drawn]**
8. **Save niches.** Each save point whose arrival point lies in a revealed cell is drawn as a niche mark at that point. The niche named by the respawn point (Save points rule 4) is highlighted. A niche in an unrevealed cell is not drawn, outlined region or not. **[Owner, 2026-11-20: marked once seen; Save points Open Question 7]**
9. **The Seeker.** The Seeker is drawn as a mark at its world position (room origin plus local position, rooms and transitions rule 2) at the map's scale, and the room it is in is highlighted. The current room comes from Rooms and transitions; World state holds no current room (world state rule 1). **[Owner, 2026-11-20]**
10. **Nothing else.** The map does not mark items, keys, pickups (the region map included), gates' state, bosses, enemies, hazards or what a secret holds (level design rule 23). **[Sourced: level design rule 23]**

**Opening and using the map**

11. **Opening.** Pressing the `map` action while the Seeker is in normal play (Save points States: Playing; not dead, resting, arriving, entering a door or in a transition) opens the map screen. Opening freezes the game with the same freeze as a transition (rooms and transitions rule 9): physics, enemies, hazards, projectiles, timers and the Seeker stop, and so do animations, particles and the camera (a shake included), and no input reaches the Seeker. A hit-pause is not held: it runs on real time (`enemy_base.gd` `_hit_stop` sets `Engine.time_scale` and restores it to 1.0 from a real-time timer), so one that runs out while the map is open is simply over, and the rest of one still running plays after closing. The freeze therefore must not set or depend on `Engine.time_scale`, which the hit-pause resets on its own. **[Owner, review 2026-11-20: let it end]** The press is read after that frame's reveals (rule 2), so the cell holding the Seeker's centre is revealed before the map opens. This system owns that freeze: no pause exists yet (Pause and menus is Vertical Slice), and Pause and menus adopts it, not the reverse. A press while not in normal play does nothing and is not buffered. The map can be opened in a fight; it freezes the fight as a transition does. **[Owner, 2026-11-20: its own button, full screen; Rec: when it can open]**
12. **Closing.** Pressing `map` again, or `cancel`, closes it, and play resumes on the next frame. Presses made while the map was open, the closing press included, are discarded, so closing the map never jumps, attacks or dashes. A button released while the map was open reads as not held on the first frame after closing; one still held reads as held (rooms and transitions rule 9: held input carries on). A direction still held when the map closes carries on as a run, as any held input does. **[Rec]**
13. **Looking around.** The map opens centred on the Seeker's mark. Move pans it. The **view centre** (the point at the screen's centre) is clamped to the bounding box of the drawn cells (revealed or outlined) grown by one cell on every side; the view's edges may go past that box, so a small map is never scrolled out of sight. Opening centres on the Seeker's mark, which lies in a revealed cell and so is always inside the clamp. The pan moves `pan_speed` cells a second along each axis independently, scaled by the stick's tilt, so a diagonal is faster than a straight pan. The pan is kept as a float, so a light tilt still moves it, and is rounded only when drawn (F1). There is no zoom in the core build (fixed scale, F1). **[Rec]**
14. **The map writes nothing.** Opening, panning and closing the map change no state. Every fact it draws is read when it opens and while it is open: revealed cells, `collected`, rooms and their exits, save points, the respawn point, the current room. **[Rec]**

### States and Transitions

**The map screen:**

| State | Meaning | Enter when | Leave when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Closed | Play runs; reveals are recorded (rule 2) | The game starts; the map is closed (rule 12) | `map` pressed in normal play (rule 11): to Open |
| Open | Full-screen map; the game frozen; input pans or closes | From Closed (rule 11) | `map` or `cancel` pressed (rule 12): to Closed. Nothing else closes it |

**A map cell:**

| State | Drawn as | Enter when | Leave when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unknown | Nothing | Start of a new game | Its region map is collected, and it is not a cell of a secret room: to Outlined. It is revealed (rule 2): to Revealed |
| Outlined | Faint outline along its room's bounds (rule 4) | Its region map is collected | It is revealed: to Revealed |
| Revealed | Filled, with its exits, door marks, markers and niches (rules 3, 5, 6, 8) | The Seeker's centre is in it, or `reveal_cover` of it is on camera (rule 2) | Never: forward only (world state rule 3) |

**An exit:** Hidden (rule 7) → Marked (drawn, its far side not seen, rule 6) → Drawn (both sides seen). An exit that is not hidden starts at Marked when it is drawn (rule 5) with its far side unseen. A hidden exit goes from Hidden to Marked when its way is opened or its far side is seen, or straight to Drawn if both sides are seen by then. None moves backward.

## Formulas

`systems-designer` lens applied in this session (lean mode spawns no agent): every variable was put at its minimum, its maximum and 0. Two inputs broke a formula and are closed by the data check below: `map_shrink` 0 divides by zero and a value that does not divide 480 and 270 gives fractional cells; `reveal_cover` 0 reveals every cell the view merely touches. No feedback loop: nothing the map draws feeds back into play. The screen size is rooms and transitions' (rule 1, 480 x 270), equal to the viewport (`project.godot`: 480 x 270, integer scaling), so the camera's view is exactly one screen. A **map pixel** is one pixel of that 480 x 270 canvas; the planned move to a 1920 x 1080 display (terrain pipeline rule 1, its Open Question 9) keeps the view at 480 x 270 game px but changes what a map pixel is (Open Questions 12).

### F1. Map position

The map_pos formula is defined as:

`map_pos = round((room_origin x screen + local_pos) / map_shrink - pan)`

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| room_origin | int pair (cells) | any world-grid cell | data file (room data) | The room's world position, its top-left cell (rooms and transitions rule 2) |
| screen | int pair (px) | 480 x 270 | constant | One screen, one world-grid cell (rooms and transitions rule 1) |
| local_pos | float pair (px) | 0 to the room's size | calculated | A point in the room: the Seeker, a niche's arrival point, an exit's anchor (rule 5), a cell corner |
| map_shrink | int | 10, 15 or 30; starts at 30 | data file | World pixels per map pixel. Must divide both 480 and 270 (their greatest common divisor is 30) so a cell is a whole number of map pixels: 30 gives 16 x 9 cells, 15 gives 32 x 18, 10 gives 48 x 27 **[Owner, 2026-11-20: 16 x 9 cells]** |
| pan | float pair (px) | such that the screen's centre point lies within the drawn cells' bounding box grown by one cell (rule 13) | calculated | The view's offset, kept as a float and rounded only in the formula; on opening, set so the Seeker's mark is at the screen's centre |

**Output Range:** a map pixel position. One cell is `480 / map_shrink` x `270 / map_shrink` map pixels; the 480 x 270 map screen shows `map_shrink` x `map_shrink` cells at once (30 x 30 at the starting value). A room of up to 20 screens, as the owner expects (Open Questions), is at most 320 x 9 px as a 20 x 1 hall, 16 x 180 as a 1 x 20 shaft, or 64 x 45 as a 4 x 5 cavern: always a readable box and never larger than the map screen. `map_shrink` 0 divides by zero, and a value that does not divide 480 and 270 (e.g. 20: 24 x 13.5) makes cell edges fall between pixels, so both are rejected (data check).
**Example:** the Seeker at local (700, 200) in a room at cell (3, 1): world (3 x 480 + 700, 1 x 270 + 200) = (2140, 470); at `map_shrink` 30 that is (71.3, 15.7), drawn at (71, 16) before the pan.

### F2. Reveal cover

The cover formula is defined as:

`cover(cell) = area(cell ∩ view) / area(cell)`; a cell is revealed when `cover(cell) >= reveal_cover`, or when the Seeker's centre is inside it (rule 2)

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| cell | rect (px) | one 480 x 270 cell of the current room | calculated | A cell of the room the Seeker is in |
| view | rect (px) | 480 x 270, clamped inside the room (rooms and transitions R4) | calculated | The camera's actual view this frame, its smoothed position included (Camera) |
| reveal_cover | float | 0.25-1.0, starts at 0.5 | data file | How much of a cell must be on camera at once to reveal it **[Rec]** |

**Output Range:** `cover` is 0 to 1 per cell. The view is exactly one cell's size, so the covers of the up to four cells it overlaps add up to 1. At 0.5, one cell is revealed, or two when the view sits exactly across their shared edge; at 0.25, up to four (the view centred on a grid corner); at 1.0, a cell only when the view lines up with it exactly, which happens in a one-screen room or against the room's corners, so reveals come almost only from the Seeker's centre. Below 0.25 a cell glimpsed at the edge of the screen is revealed, which tells the player about space they have barely seen; 0 reveals every cell the view touches. The view never leaves the room (R4), so nothing of a neighbour room is ever revealed from inside another. The camera follows the Seeker, so its view is centred near the Seeker's centre and, at 0.5, the cover test mostly agrees with the centre test: it reveals a cell first only when the camera leads the Seeker (look-ahead and fall lead, `player_camera.gd`), by up to the lead's distance. Below 0.5 it reveals the cells ahead before the Seeker reaches them; that is the range where this knob changes what the player sees. Secret cells ignore the cover test (rule 2).
**Example:** in a 3 x 2 room, the Seeker's centre at (540, 235) and the view's top-left at (300, 100): it covers 180 x 170 of cell (0, 0) (cover 0.24), 300 x 170 of (1, 0) (0.39), 180 x 100 of (0, 1) (0.14) and 300 x 100 of (1, 1) (0.23). None reaches 0.5, so only the cell holding the Seeker's centre, (1, 0), is revealed this frame; once the view moves to (240, 0) with the Seeker's centre at (500, 135), cell (0, 0) reaches 0.5 and is revealed too.

**Data file and check:** `map_shrink`, `reveal_cover` and `pan_speed` (cells a second along each axis while move is held, scaled by the stick's tilt, 15-60, starts at 30: a 30-cell map screen crosses in about 1 s at `map_shrink` 30; at 15 or 10 a map screen holds 15 or 10 cells and crosses in 0.5 or 0.33 s, so `pan_speed` is retuned whenever `map_shrink` changes; below 15 a region feels slow to scan, above 60 a tap overshoots a room) live in `src/ui/map/map_config.tres`, a `MapConfig` resource **[Rec]**. Its `validate()` returns the name of every value outside its range (`map_shrink` not 10, 15 or 30; `reveal_cover` below 0.25 or above 1.0; `pan_speed` below 15 or above 60); it runs when the config loads at boot, and a non-empty result stops the game before the title screen and names the values, as Save points does. **[Rec]**

## Edge Cases

`systems-designer` not consulted: Lean mode. Review manually before production.

**Revealing**
- **If the Seeker's centre lies exactly on the edge between two cells**: it is in the cell to the right, or below; a cell spans its left and top edges, not its right and bottom ones. **[Rec]**
- **If the Seeker crosses more than one cell in a frame by moving inside one room** (a fast fall, a launch, a position set by code within the room): every cell its centre passed through between the two frames is revealed, not only the one it ends in, so a shaft never shows a gap. **A teleport is not a crossing**: a door trip, a respawn, a load and fast travel put the Seeker at an arrival point somewhere else (rooms and transitions rules 7, 8) and reveal only the cell holding that point, never the cells between where it was and where it arrives, so a respawn never fills in the way to the niche or touches a secret cell on the way (rule 7). **[Rec; review 2026-11-20: scope of the sweep]**
- **If the Seeker is dead, resting, arriving or entering a door**: reveals go on while the world runs, since being on camera is seeing, not touching (Save points Edge Cases; level design States). During a transition's freeze nothing moves, so nothing is revealed; the arrival reveals its cell on the first frame of the fade-in (rule 2). **[Rec]**
- **If a save was made before revealed cells existed** (a room is in rooms visited but has no revealed cells): the map draws that room as if every cell were revealed, except the secret cells of a larger room (rule 7), which stay unrevealed because nothing records that they were found; a visited secret room is drawn whole, since visiting it is finding it. Opening the map writes nothing. The first time the Seeker reveals a cell in that room, the record is seeded with every cell the map was drawing as revealed, so the room never shrinks on the map (forward only, rule 2). **[Rec; world state rule 9; review 2026-11-20: secret cells kept]**
- **If a room is resized or cut after release**: revealed cells are counted from the room's top-left (rule 2), so a room that has shipped is grown only at its right or bottom edge; growing it at the top or left would shift every kept cell onto a different screen. That is a requirement on Level design (Open Questions 3). A cell that a bigger room has gained starts unrevealed. Revealed cells outside the room's new size, and those of a cut room, are kept in the record and not drawn (world state rule 10, as extended by Open Questions 1). **[Rec, accepted by owner, review 2026-11-20: right and bottom only]**
- **If the map opens during a hit-pause**: the world is frozen as in rule 11 for as long as the map is open; the hit-pause runs on real time and may run out meanwhile, and the map's freeze does not depend on `Engine.time_scale`, so its reset to 1.0 unfreezes nothing. **[Owner, review 2026-11-20: let it end]**
- **If many cells are revealed in a short time** (a fall down a tall shaft): each is a record change committed at the end of its frame (world state rule 6), at most one commit a frame. The commit count is World state's to measure (its Open Question 2). **[Rec]**

**The region map and secrets**
- **If a region map is collected while rooms of its region are partly revealed**: their revealed cells stay filled and the rest are outlined (rule 4). **[Rec]**
- **If a region has no region map, or more than one**: the room check rejects the region and names it, a requirement on level design (Dependencies). **[Rec]**
- **If a secret room or secret section is partly revealed**: its revealed cells are drawn; its unrevealed cells draw nothing of their own. A secret room is not outlined by a region map; a secret section of a larger room shows only its room's rectangle (rule 4). **[Owner, 2026-11-20; review 2026-11-20: a full rectangle]**
- **If a secret section is on camera from outside it** (behind a breakable wall in a larger room): its cells are not revealed, however much of them is on screen, until the Seeker's centre enters them (rule 2). **[Rec]**
- **If a hidden exit's far side is revealed first** (the secret was reached another way): the exit is drawn, and it carries a marker until its near side is seen (rules 5-7), so the map points back at the room beyond; the hiding wall stays as world state has it. **[Owner, review 2026-11-20: a marker]**
- **If the region map lies in a secret room**: it outlines the region as usual and still never outlines a secret room, the one it was found in included; that room shows the cells already revealed. **[Rec]**
- **If an opening's midpoint lies exactly on a cell edge** (a span centred on a screen's edge): the anchor belongs to the cell right of or below it, as for the Seeker's centre. Only where the line is drawn depends on it; which sides count as seen does not (rule 5). **[Rec]**

**Exits and marks**
- **If only one side of an open edge is seen**: the exit is drawn from that side with a marker (rule 6). Once both sides are seen it is drawn once, with no marker. **[Rec]**
- **If the Seeker crosses an exit away from its anchor** (a wide opening, or one that crosses a screen's edge): the cell it arrives in holds a point of the opening's span, so that side is seen and the marker goes, though the anchor's cell stays unrevealed. The line is still drawn at the anchor. **[Rec; review 2026-11-20]**
- **If an opening's cells on one side mix secret and non-secret cells**: the room check rejects it (rule 7). **[Rec]**
- **If a door's pair is in the same room**: the pair's side is the paired door's anchor cell and arrival cell in that room; the marker goes when either is revealed. **[Rec]**
- **If an exit is behind a gate the Seeker cannot cross yet**: it keeps its marker until its far side is seen (rule 6), so the map keeps pointing at it. **[Rec; level design rule 8]**
- **If the respawn point names a save point that no longer exists, or one in an unrevealed cell**: no niche is highlighted; Save points picks the arrival (its rule 9). **[Rec]**
- **If the Seeker is past the room's bounds**: this should not happen while the map is open, since the map cannot open in a transition (rule 11) and rooms and transitions rule 6 keeps the centre inside the live room. As a guard against bad data, its mark is drawn at the nearest point on the room's bounds. **[Rec]**

**Opening and closing**
- **If `map` is pressed on the frame a hit brings health to zero**: the hit is resolved first; the Seeker is dead, not in normal play, and the map does not open (rule 11). **[Rec]**
- **Once Pause and menus exists, if `map` is pressed while paused, or pause is pressed while the map is open**: the press is ignored; pause and the map never stack. Until then there is no pause, and the map's freeze (rule 11) is the only one. **[Rec]**
- **If the game is quit or crashes with the map open**: nothing is lost and nothing is written by the map (rule 14). Cells revealed before it opened were committed in their frames. **[Rec]**
- **If only the start room is drawn**: the view's centre is clamped to that room plus one cell (rule 13), so the map cannot be scrolled out of sight of it. **[Rec]**
- **If a drawn region has no map colour in its data**: the room check rejects it (a requirement on level design). **[Rec]**

## Visual/Audio Requirements

`art-director` lens applied in this session (lean mode spawns no agent). There is no art bible yet (`design/art/art-bible.md` does not exist), so this section follows the approved visual direction, `design/narrative/kiln-city-visual-direction.md`; the art bible, once written, owns every look named here. `audio-director` not consulted: Lean mode.

- **Ground and fill.** The map's ground is the world's ground: soot-umber, low value. A revealed cell is filled in its region's map colour at low to mid value, never brighter than the lines on it. **[Rec; visual direction: Palette and light]**
- **Lines are the brightest thing.** Room borders, exit lines and door marks are the brightest values on the map, as play-layer collision edges are in a room; one pixel wide, on whole pixels (F1). **[Rec; visual direction]**
- **No key hues, ever.** Orange, copper-green, chalk-yellow and violet are reserved for the four keys and used for nothing else; the map never marks a key (rule 10), so it never uses them, and no region's map colour may be one of them. The art bible names the four hues' values and how close a map colour may come to one; until it exists this is checked by eye at each region's grey-box review, not by the room check, and no automated criterion is set. **[Sourced: visual direction, reserved hues; Rec: the check]**
- **The Seeker's mark** is a bone-pale hollow square (a one-pixel outline with a clear centre), the lightest matte value on the map, as the Seeker is on screen, so a niche or any mark at its point shows through; its room is lifted one step in value. **[Rec; Owner, review 2026-11-20: hollow]**
- **Marks read by shape, not colour alone.** The unexplored marker, the door mark, the niche mark and the Seeker's mark each have a distinct shape, so the map reads in greyscale and for colour-blind players. The respawn niche is highlighted by brightness, not by a reserved hue. **[Rec]**
- **Outlines.** A room outlined by the region map (rule 4) is a dithered one-pixel line in the region's colour, with no fill, clearly fainter than a revealed border. On the first opening after the region map is collected, its new outlines draw in over 0.5 s, so the find is noticed. **[Rec]**
- **In play, almost nothing.** A cell revealed in play shows no pop-up, flash or sound; the map simply holds it next time (as transitions are quiet, level design rule 21). The one exception is finding a region map (next). **[Rec]**
- **Finding a region map.** The pickup shows a one-line message, "[Region name] map found", and plays the map's rustle cue; the outlines draw in on the next open (rule 4). Without a cue the pickup changes nothing the player can see, and a player who seldom opens the map would never learn what it did. The line's look and duration belong to HUD / UX (Open Questions). **[Owner, 2026-11-20: short text line plus the rustle]**
- **Audio.** Opening plays a short dry rustle, like ash sifted across a slate; closing plays it reversed. The world's sounds stop with the freeze; music keeps playing. **[Rec]**
- **Mark sizes and stacking.** At 30 world pixels to a map pixel a niche is under one pixel, so each mark is drawn at a fixed size in map pixels, not scaled from the world. Starting sizes: the Seeker's mark 5 x 5 (hollow), a niche 3 x 3 (filled), a door 3 x 5, an unexplored marker 3 x 3 (hollow), an exit line 3 long across the border. The UX spec and the art bible confirm the shapes and sizes. Marks that overlap stack, top first: the Seeker, the respawn niche, niches, doors, unexplored markers, exit lines. No mark is dropped to make room for another, and the Seeker's mark is hollow so what lies at its point stays visible. **[Rec]**

## Game Feel

- **It is there the moment you ask.** The map is fully drawn on the first frame after the press and gone on the first frame after closing: no open or close animation, no load (the AC's one-frame budget). The only motion is the outlines' 0.5 s draw-in after a region map is found. **[Rec]**
- **Three answers at a glance.** Opened centred on the Seeker, the map answers where am I, where have I not been (markers) and where would a death send me (the highlighted niche), without panning. **[Owner, 2026-11-20: position and niches shown]**
- **The world fills in as you walk it.** Big rooms are drawn screen by screen, so a cavern half-crossed is half on the map, and the gaps are places to go back to. **[Owner, 2026-11-20: screen by screen, Ori-style]**
- **Panning is quick.** A held direction pans `pan_speed` cells a second, 30 at the start, so a full screen of map crosses in about 1 s. **[Rec]**
- **Playtest question.** Whether finding the region map feels like a reward or like a chore the player must do before the map is useful (Acceptance Criteria, [Play]).

## UI Requirements

- **The map screen.** Full screen, 480 x 270, over the frozen game (not shown behind it). It holds the map (rules 1-10), the name of the region under the screen's centre in the top-left corner (nothing when no drawn cell is under it), and one line of control glyphs at the bottom ("Map / Back: close", "Move: look"). No other text and no legend in the core build; the marks are few enough to learn by use (Open Questions). **[Rec]**
- **Input.** `map` opens and closes; `ui_cancel` closes; move pans. No mouse panning and no zoom in the core build (Open Questions). The map holds no focusable controls, so Godot 4.6's separate mouse and keyboard focus does not apply to it. **[Rec]**
- **No minimap in play.** The HUD shows no map during play in the core build (Open Questions). **[Rec]**
- **Accessibility.** Marks read by shape (Visual/Audio). Whether a screen reader can describe a custom-drawn map is not sourceable from `docs/engine-reference/godot/modules/ui.md`, which covers Control nodes only (Open Questions). **[Rec]**
