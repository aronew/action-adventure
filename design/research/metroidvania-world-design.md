# Shape the world, then submerge the locks

The best metroidvanias are built space-first and lock-second. Team Cherry says it designs around "the Citadel at the top of the world, and what's around it" rather than "which power up you are getting next", and that it tries to "submerge" lock-and-key "in favour of a more naturalistic world". The games that read cleanly then constrain that space with a few hard rules. They change topology by act: a funnel, then a three-way fork, then a remix of familiar ground. Each key opens few locks and has one visual signature (Metroid Dread). A save sits before every boss and never more than about seven screens from the last one (Ender Lilies, Blasphemous). The world is held to a memory budget: Team Cherry cut a whole biome because players could not hold more map in their heads. This adds a layer to your own four pillars and three loops: the sources treat the map, the saves and the shortcuts as **memory tools**, and they treat a key's worth as **how few gates it opens and how many fights it changes**. On presentation, the documented AAA recipe is a foreground / play / multi-layer background split, painted pixel art relit through normal maps (Dead Cells), and an authored camera. Godot 4.7 has every node this needs, and your project is already on Forward+ with integer viewport scaling. The design rules at the end apply this to the Seeker. They show which of the Seeker's abilities make safe early keys and which would leak through your gates, using jump and dash distances computed from your own `player_config.tres`. They are labelled **[Sourced]**, **[Recommendation]** or **[Calc]** (derived from your config).

A caveat on evidence. Primary designer statements are strong for Team Cherry, Billy Basso (Animal Well), MercurySteam and Nintendo (Dread), Ubisoft Montpellier (Memory Shards), Moon Studios, The Game Kitchen (Blasphemous) and the Ender Lilies team. For Nine Sols, Aeterna Noctis and Axiom Verge's topology, only reviews, guides and fan sources turned up. The 2017 PC Gamer Team Cherry interview and the Game Maker's Toolkit (GMTK) transcripts were read only through search excerpts, because the full pages were blocked. Fan counts are flagged where they appear.

## Topology changes by act: funnel, three-way fork, remix

Your "organic web from a central crossroads" describes Hollow Knight's finished shape. The sources show that **the openness of that web is scheduled, not constant**. GMTK's analysis of Silksong describes Act 1 as a near-linear "giant U-bend" through eight areas (Moss Grotto to Shellwood) "with little zig-zagging", with a fixed boss sequence and only a few optional skips (the Fleas caravan, a Deep Docks door, a Wormways door, Sinner's Road). Act 2 opens into **three melodies collectable in any order** and a Clawline reachable by two routes. Act 3 returns to a linear Abyss dive, then opens again with a **"3 of 4 hearts"** goal. Mark Brown calls this a **"concertina structure"** that alternates momentum and discovery ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-world-design-of-hollow-knight)). Hollow Knight shows the same arc at smaller scale. Early reach is limited to Dirtmouth and the Forgotten Crossroads. Late game, the hub turns into the Infected Crossroads, which **blocks well-worn routes and adds new friction without new geometry** ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-to-design-a-great-metroidvania-map), search excerpt). That gives a measurable knob, *simultaneous valid objectives*, which runs 1 → 3 → "3 of 4" in Silksong. Pellen gives the target in his own words: "there could be **three or four valid directions**… two of those directions might lead to a new ability, but the other two might lead to entirely new parts of the world". Team Cherry's difficulty advice depends on it, since a stuck player "can always turn around and see what awaits down a different path" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/action/hollow-knight-devs-highlight-a-big-difference-between-their-sequel-and-most-metroidvanias-the-players-choice-about-where-to-go-in-silksong-is-not-dictated-by-what-power-ups-you-currently-have)).

Hubs come in two kinds, and the difference matters for a Nine Sols-inspired game. Hollow Knight's Crossroads is a **geographic hub** that you cross on foot. Nine Sols' Four Seasons Pavilion is a **warp hub**, "the hub area connecting all major zones", reached through Root Node fast travel ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/how-to-fast-travel-in-nine-sols)). A warp hub gives story and vendors an anchor without forcing the map to be radial. It also explains a criticism you should take seriously: aggregated Steam reviews call Nine Sols' level design **"too linear for a Metroidvania"** ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1809540/nine_sols.md)), and GamesRadar wanted more guidance through its world ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/action/nine-sols-review)). If you take Nine Sols' transit-sector shape, borrow Hollow Knight's cross-connections between sectors, or the web collapses into spokes.

Large webs stay legible through a **world axis** and through **region silhouettes**. Silksong's single goal, the Citadel above everything, organises the map vertically. Its movement keys serve "go up": Drifter's Cloak for updrafts, Cling Grip, and the Faydown Cloak for Mount Fay. The Abyss then reverses the direction ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-world-design-of-hollow-knight)). Ender Lilies' developers designed the upper and lower halves so players can tell them apart at a glance: flooded and frightening below, dangerous enemies at the castle top. **Vertical position alone tells the player where they are** ([Game*Spark interview](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2021/09/05/111615_2.html)). Ario Barzan's analysis of Symphony of the Night finds that **"nearly half of the map is negative space"**. He finds that each area has a distinct outline: the Outer Wall is "mostly one vertical block", the Catacombs "never higher than two rooms", and the Clock Tower and Long Library are "two large chunks bookending" smaller bits. He also identifies a stated "rule" of building subsidiary spaces around primary ones ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/economy-and-thematic-structure-symphony-of-the-night-s-level-design)). Super Metroid adds the region boundary marker: elevators "are usually reserved for passages between different zones". It also adds a safety net: the game always provides **"a back door"**, so crucial elements are eventually met even off the intended path ([Game Developer, "The Invisible Hand of Super Metroid"](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-invisible-hand-of-super-metroid)).

The hardest ceiling is **memory, not content**. Team Cherry cut Bone Forest, Hollow Knight's largest planned area, because "you have to keep a lot of it in your head, and potentially another very, very large area was just pushing people too far". Kingdom's Edge became a catch-all that existed partly "for map balance" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-to-design-a-great-metroidvania-map)). Hollow Knight's original prototype route gave one power-up per biome, with return visits to "city 2" and the fungus. Its game-jam idea of prefab rooms that "rejig" themselves was dropped: "It was a headache. It wasn't fun." Gibson's coherence rule is that the world is "98% pure", with no paradox rooms, because "it's super annoying to get to a point that's 2 centimeters from somewhere you want to be and you can't get there" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-to-design-a-great-metroidvania-map); attribution from excerpts). The most useful planning numbers come from Animal Well. Its world is a **16×16 grid of 256 rooms**, because each room had a one-byte ID. Basso designed **500+ rooms that were cut or "altered beyond recognition"**, and each new room took "five or six iterations" of play ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/platformer/solo-dev-behind-breakout-metroidvania-hit-animal-well-says-his-game-has-256-encounters-but-he-had-to-cut-at-least-500-of-them-during-development); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/the-scratch-coding-and-discipline-at-the-heart-of-animal-well)). Ori and the Will of the Wisps has "300+ levels", and Mahler calls the whole game "one huge puzzle" where a change on one side of the map breaks a spot on the other ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-designing-the-gorgeous-metroidvania-i-ori-and-the-will-of-the-wisps-i-)). A fan rebuild counts Hollow Knight at 307 rooms (unverified; [SVG](https://www.svg.com/366885/the-untold-truth-of-hollow-knight/)). Aeterna Noctis shows what happens without that discipline. It advertises 16 connected areas and runs about 50 hours. Reviewers called every area "exasperatingly big" and the backtracking "an obnoxious amount" ([Wireframe](https://wireframe.raspberrypi.com/articles/aeterna-noctis-review-a-hard-days-night-and-then-some); [FingerGuns](https://fingerguns.net/reviews/2023/02/20/aeterna-noctis-review-ps5-symphony-for-the-devil/)).

Two generative philosophies conflict here, and a designer should choose between them on purpose. Team Cherry is **space-first**: "we establish the foundations, and as those become clearer, the world suggests what else is going to exist" ([ACMI](https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/from-ludum-dare-to-pharloom/)). Basso is **teach-first**: he builds item-centred puzzle sections that "can be played in isolation" and lets the map "grow roots on it" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/the-scratch-coding-and-discipline-at-the-heart-of-animal-well)). Hollow Knight's own history combines them. Pellen describes fixing a rough ability order and the world shape together, then moving "that power-up progression from a linear thing to section it out". Gibson describes chipping away "a lot of the hard gating… to the point where a lot of those power-ups ended up being optional". Speedrunners now finish with "barely any of the power-ups. **Which is exactly what we had hoped**" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-to-design-a-great-metroidvania-map)). Even Silksong had to correct its opening: Team Cherry added Moss Grotto after finding The Marrow "a brutal introduction". It teaches "this is how far you can jump, here are some spikes… here's a hidden room" through play, not prompts ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/action/silksongs-first-area-was-only-added-after-team-cherry-realized-it-needed-a-softer-intro-were-trying-to-be-kind-of-respectful-to-the-player-were-not-trying-to-baby-them)).

## Good keys open few locks, change fights, and come late if they fly

Your rule that keys are verbs gets a sharper test from Dread. Mark Brown's analysis argues the game avoids getting players lost by **reducing "the number of locks each key opens"** and placing **"the most important lock near the key that opens it"**, so the Plasma Beam does not unlock "a flood of doors". By contrast, he notes that in Super Metroid "after getting the missiles... there's literally only 13 rooms to check" ([GMTK transcript](https://amara.org/subtitles/Aci4iDbgsAtY/en/1/download/Why%20You%20Didnt%20Get%20Lost%20in%20Metroid%20Dread.en.txt), snippet). Dread backs this with **one material signature per key**: orange doors for Charge, "blue, electric-looking strips" for the Spider Magnet, green coverings for Super Missile, purple covers for Wave, "large flaming mushrooms" for Ice Missile, and blue-triangle tiles for Grapple. The boss "door-blocking menace" doubles as a refill station before each fight ([Gameranx](https://gameranx.com/features/id/274690/article/metroid-dread-where-to-find-every-suit-upgrade-progression-locations-guide/); [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/metroid-dread-items-guide)). Silksong makes the same point quietly: each ability usually opens only a few locks, with the next lock nearby. Cling Grip is the named exception that opens many ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-world-design-of-hollow-knight)). Hollow Knight's Mantis Claw does the same job; Brown reads it as the hinge after which the map "opens up considerably" ([Boss Keys transcript](https://amara.org/subtitles/1YBjcP2T6VPf/en/1/download/The%20World%20Design%20of%20Hollow%20Knight%20%20Boss%20Keys.en.txt), snippet). The lesson is that **a key that opens many gates is a pacing event**. Place it where you want the map to burst open, not as routine progression.

There is a tension between Dread's principle and your rule that an ability is never placed right in front of its gate. They fit together. Dread's "near" lock is the *first use* after pickup, your sandbox escape. The remembered gate, seen hours earlier, is the payoff. Ubisoft formalises the near lock as a **"learning workshop highlighting blockers similar to those he met previously"** right after each new ability, with timed challenges built in "short-term" frameworks "to remove friction in case of failure" ([GameRant, Mounir Radi](https://gamerant.com/prince-persia-lostcrown-metroidvania-gameplay-interview/)). Super Metroid links each new tool to a known hazard. After the Ice Beam, a flier from the "shaft of no return" can be frozen into a platform over lava. Two Grapple tutorial rooms precede a Crateria room that needs the technique ([Invisible Hand](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-invisible-hand-of-super-metroid)).

No designer in the sources explains *why* this order holds; that is a documented gap. The mechanical reason is a safe inference: a double jump invalidates every ledge between one and two jump-heights, everywhere in the world at once. Dread's designers made the same kind of call with Scan Pulse, the tool that reveals hidden blocks. Jacobo Luengo says it was "so useful for exploration, which makes it very tempting to use constantly", so they **moved it later and changed it** rather than taxing it with Aeion cost, because the tax would have discouraged other Aeion use ([Shinesparkers interview](https://shinesparkers.net/interviews/jacobo-luengo/)). The rule: a key that trivialises exploration goes late. Don't tax it.

Where keys come from has more texture than your three channels (boss, shrine, mentor) suggest. By guide counts, **exploration pickups dominate**. Hollow Knight gives 2 of 6 movement keys from bosses and 4 from finds. Dread gives about 4–5 from bosses, 5–6 from E.M.M.I. kills, and about 11–12 from Chozo statues and pedestals. Silksong spreads 9 abilities across 2 bosses, 3 shrines or gauntlets, 1 NPC quest (the Seamstress's Drifter's Cloak) and story beats ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/silksong-abilities-skills-powers); [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/metroid-dread-items-guide)). Dread adds a fourth channel worth stealing. In an E.M.M.I. zone, the player first *flees* an unkillable hunter, then gets a **temporary one-shot Omega Blaster**, and the kill converts into a **permanent new verb**, all inside the zone whose layout they just learned under pressure ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metroid_Dread)). Sakamoto's design constraint for that loop applies to every post-pickup test room: "when a player hits a game over, **they know what to try on their next attempt**" ([Nintendo Wire](https://nintendowire.com/news/2022/01/24/metroid-dread-producer-discusses-the-games-difficulty-and-why-the-e-m-m-i-encounters-were-so-unforgiving/)). The same channel has a fatigue limit: one critic said E.M.M.I.s "stop being scary and start being annoying" about halfway through ([Nintendo Life](https://3dsvc.nintendolife.com/news/2022/03/am2r-creator-isnt-a-fan-of-metroid-dreads-e-m-m-i-encounters)).

The documented failures sharpen your keys-as-verbs pillar. Blasphemous's relics "cause the environment to react to you, rather than affect your ability to play", and unlocking them "has you backtracking in a boring way" ([Metroidvania Review](https://metroidvaniareview.com/blasphemous/)). In other words, **a key that only toggles the world is a door, not a verb**. Aeterna Noctis's wall jump was called "temperamental", with the complaint that a key's use rules "should be explained rather than left to chance" ([FingerGuns](https://fingerguns.net/reviews/2023/02/20/aeterna-noctis-review-ps5-symphony-for-the-devil/), attribution approximate). Basso's critique of Dread hits the optional layer hardest. Missile tanks are pickups where "I already know ahead of time what I'm going to get… It'll just see a number go up". The core kit was "the same item set you get in all the Metroid games", following "the blueprint, give you the double jump and a dash" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/features/i-avoided-almost-all-the-existing-tropes-peeling-back-the-layers-of-animal-well)). The Seeker's kit *is* that blueprint, so its novelty has to come from what the verbs do in fights, which is Nine Sols' answer. Nine Sols turns deflection into Qi for talisman dashes and makes the Tai-Chi Kick an air parry ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Sols)). Ori's Bash makes enemy projectiles into launch anchors ([SDA KB](https://kb.speeddemosarchive.com/Ori/Skills_and_Abilities)).

Item and knowledge gates follow two rules in the sources. **Item gates stay few and point at optional content**: Hollow Knight has four Simple Keys for four doors, and Silksong's Apostate Key opens a cell leading to an optional boss and skill ([game-checklists](https://game-checklists.com/hollow-knight/112-checklist/); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/silksong-abilities-skills-powers)). **Knowledge gates must sit on a baseline the game has taught**. Basso designs "for three different people at any given point". He insists a secret "needs to break from the pattern that even the current game you're playing established". His reply to calls for secret markers is "they wouldn't be secrets then… You're not entitled to get everything" ([Thinky Games](https://thinkygames.com/features/interview-how-animal-well-is-using-secrets-and-mysteries-to-be-a-different-kind-of-metroidvania/); [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/features/i-avoided-almost-all-the-existing-tropes-peeling-back-the-layers-of-animal-well)). He still had to build "something that was broadly accessible and fun" as the base first ([Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/the-making-of-animal-well-2024s-most-unique-metroidvania)). Tom Happ makes the same argument for Axiom Verge's glitch gun: "the beaten path of the game exists just as an established baseline to allow off-the-beaten-path exploration… You might even want to include traditional elements so that can provide context for when you later subvert them" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/metroidvania-interview-tom-happ)). The counterexample comes from Silksong. Its Silk and Soul quest needs the Faydown Cloak, a Bellhome key, 22 fleas and 17 quest points, and those prerequisites "are not discoverable in-game" ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-world-design-of-hollow-knight)). That is a knowledge gate on the critical-adjacent path that the game never taught.

Sequence breaks are a design budget, not a bug class. Mahler says physics-driven breaks are "a matter of tweaking things until the Design Department is happy", and Moon **hired two top Ori speedrunners** as testers ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-designing-the-gorgeous-metroidvania-i-ori-and-the-will-of-the-wisps-i-)). Luengo describes a Bomb Block in Burenia placed purely to stop players getting stuck after Ghavoran, and calls Dread's early bomb-Kraid break deliberate: it "rewards a feat players may have already achieved" ([Shinesparkers](https://shinesparkers.net/interviews/jacobo-luengo/)). Whether Super Metroid's breaks were intended is disputed in fan sources, and a quote attributed to programmer Yasuhiko Fujii is unverified.

For the optional economy, the benchmark arithmetic is **4 shards per health unit**: Hollow Knight has 16 mask shards (up to 9 masks) and Silksong 20 (5 extra masks). Magic comes in 9–18 fragments. A build layer of about 40–50+ items sits on top: Hollow Knight's charms, Silksong's 52 tools. Shards arrive through shops unlocked by item keys, rescue-count milestones and quests, not only hidden rooms ([game-checklists HK](https://game-checklists.com/hollow-knight/112-checklist/); [game-checklists Silksong](https://game-checklists.com/silksong/100-percent.html)). Gibson's definition of discovery is the brief for those rewards: not "500 flags… 500 coins" but "unique sights, unique characters, unique systems, like a new shopkeeper" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-to-design-a-great-metroidvania-map)).

## Saves, shortcuts and maps work as memory prosthetics

The spacing numbers converge from independent sources. Blasphemous's lead level designer Enrique Colinet says Prie Dieu altars sit about one per area and are **"never more than seven screens apart"**. Boss fights are placed **next to shortcuts** so that afterwards players can "go straight to wherever they want to be" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-blasphemous-i-level-design-iterates-on-classic-metroidvanias)). Ender Lilies' level designer Yokoyama: "we always place a save point before each boss" ([Game*Spark](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2021/09/05/111615_2.html)). Hollow Knight's checklist count of **52 benches** ([game-checklists](https://game-checklists.com/hollow-knight/benches-checklist/)) against the fan count of 307 rooms gives roughly **one bench per six rooms**. That is close to Blasphemous's ceiling, though both figures are unofficial. The run-back debate is real and unresolved. One critic says Silksong has more benches than Hollow Knight, and nearer ones ([Elliot C Smith](https://www.elliotcsmith.com/get-good-is-silksong-too-hard/)), which suggests Team Cherry loosened its own rule. Others argue for a bench outside every boss ([Epilogue Gaming](https://epiloguegaming.com/?p=7321)). Super Metroid adds three placement rules from Hugo Bille's analysis. A save sits right after the one-way Wall Jump drop, so a trapped player is never trapped far from a save. A save sits by the elevator to Ridley ("You are going to need it"). And the Wrecked Ship's save station **refuses service** until Phantoon is dead, so the region's state lives in its save point ([Invisible Hand](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-invisible-hand-of-super-metroid)). Dread places invisible checkpoints on **both sides of each E.M.M.I. zone**, before and after bosses, and after inter-region travel (fan analysis via the GMTK transcript, snippet). Ori shows the alternative and its retreat. Blind Forest's player-placed Soul Links cost energy. For Will of the Wisps, Moon replaced them with checkpoints, partly because the team could not solve "how you remind people to put down a Soul Link". Mahler's verdict: "Nobody missed" them ([VideoGamer](https://www.videogamer.com/soul-links-in-ori-and-the-will-of-the-wisps-didnt-make-sense-says-developer)).

Shortcut devices need variety. Blasphemous's first shortcut mechanic, one-use levers, was **"overused" and cluttered the screen**, so many became breakable glass cases smashed with the sword. Reusable lifts and teleports cover district-scale detours, and layout moved from whiteboard to post-its to Photoshop "specifically to plan shortcuts" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-blasphemous-i-level-design-iterates-on-classic-metroidvanias)). Fast travel is deliberately sparser than saving. Hollow Knight has **11 Stag stations** against 52 benches, most behind one-time Geo tolls such as Crossroads 50 and Greenpath 140, which ties the economy to access ([Fextralife](https://hollowknight.wiki.fextralife.com/Stag+Station)). Lost Crown has **56 Wak-Wak tree checkpoints against 22 fast-travel statues**, by guide count ([Guides4Gamers](https://guides4gamers.com/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown/poi/wak-wak-trees)). Silksong keeps the Citadel's pneumatic lifts separate from Pharloom's Bellways, linked only through the Grand Bellway, so **late regions feel far away without extra walking** ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-world-design-of-hollow-knight)). Nine Sols' range is unresolved. Guides say Root Node travel links only the Pavilion and the last node visited ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/how-to-fast-travel-in-nine-sols)). A Steam thread quotes the Fusang Horn text as allowing any node and calls the restriction a bug ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1809540/discussions/0/4333105050947440555)).

Each studio's map system answers a stated problem. Team Cherry calls keeping discovery alive while still giving a useful map "the single biggest design challenge" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-to-design-a-great-metroidvania-map), excerpt). Their answer is a map bought per zone from a wandering cartographer. In Silksong, resting at a bench fills in what you've explored, and secret walls never show on the map, which defeats Brown's "map poking" (probing unexplored map edges until something gives) ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-world-design-of-hollow-knight)). Dread's answer is **Icon Highlight**, which lights every door, block or item of one type across the map, plus a promise that hidden blocks on the main path "always have some hint" such as an enemy, a glimpse of a passage, or a sound ([Nintendo AU Dread Report Vol. 9](https://www.nintendo.com.au/news-and-articles/metroid-dread-report-vol-9-handy-tips-for-newcomers)). Players still got lost where "you had to shoot a random unmarked wall or floor" ([Tildes](https://tildes.net/~games/z02/why_you_didnt_get_lost_in_metroid_dread)). Lost Crown's Memory Shards came from a concrete failure: "We were playing metroidvanias, we took a break, and when looking at our map we forgot what we were trying to do". Each shard is a HUD-less screenshot pinned to the map. The **cap** began as a cloud-save size limit. It turned out to make players "manage them efficiently", which keeps the map "clean". Extra shards are placed in **chests next to blocked paths** to remind players the feature exists ([Game Developer, Rémi Boutin](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-memory-shards-help-players-find-secrets-in-prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown)). The starting count conflicts between two interviews with the same designer: **15** in Game Developer, **25** in Digital Trends ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown-memory-shards-development/)). Treat the number as unsettled, but the principle as solid. Ender Lilies goes the other way on purpose. Its map is abstract squares joined by lines, with red circles for unopened routes and no story guidance, "an older style", to keep exploration a challenge ([Game*Spark](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2021/09/05/111615_2.html)). Nine Sols' map is the weakness reviewers name most often ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/action/nine-sols-review)), which is the main thing **not** to inherit from your primary reference. Super Metroid's two cheapest tricks still work. Its revealed Brinstar map shows "a clearly missing connection" between east and west halves. And a three-screen lower-Brinstar tunnel is labelled "Maridia" on the auto-map hours before you can enter that region ([Invisible Hand](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-invisible-hand-of-super-metroid)).

## Rooms start from a place sentence, then a grey box, then art

Pellen gives the most usable micro-scale method in his own words: "If you're trying to build a Mario level, you might take an empty space and think, oh, should there be a few platforms here?… But if you just start thinking — well, **it's a cavern with a lake on the left or it's a ruined tower**", the design "is much easier" ([ACMI](https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/from-ludum-dare-to-pharloom/)). That is the mechanism behind your "natural, lived-in world" preference: platforms fall out of the objects named in the sentence. Moon Studios adds the gate before art. Designers "simply draw polygons that define the platforms and shapes", the world is not grid-based, and each blockout gets "harsh and honest feedback" until it earns a **"design approved" stamp**. Only then does set dressing begin ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-designing-the-gorgeous-metroidvania-i-ori-and-the-will-of-the-wisps-i-)). Korol states the combat corollary: Moon "always starts with the solids", because an enemy that isn't fun while you jump around boxes won't be fun in final art ([MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/development-news/unity-focus-making-ori-and-the-blind-forest/)). Mahler adds two quality bars: every jump should "feel really good", and stretches of holding the stick forward make a "walking simulator" ([Film Stories](https://filmstories.co.uk/gaming/ori-and-the-will-of-the-wisps-interview-making-a-masterpiece/)). One analysis observes that Hollow Knight uses **no one-way pass-through platforms**. Every surface is solid, so the route must be legible in the terrain silhouette itself. That is unconfirmed by Team Cherry ([Ludonauta](https://ludonauta.itch.io/platformer-essentials/devlog/1084669/the-hidden-genius-behind-hollow-knights-terrain-design)), but it is a strong reading of your "floors, not platforms" rule.

Hard size data exists only for the classics. A Super Metroid screen is **256×256 px, 16×16 blocks of 16 px**. Rooms span **1 to 30 screens**, and each room authors per-screen **scroll data** saying which screens the camera may enter, so parts of a room can be hidden until an event ([SMMM](https://metroidconstruction.com/SMMM/)). That is the direct ancestor of per-room Camera2D limits. Blasphemous started with large scrolling areas. In June 2018 it switched to **fixed screen-based scenes** in a district → zone → scene hierarchy, because the levels felt too big, were hard to build collaboratively, and cost performance ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-blasphemous-i-level-design-iterates-on-classic-metroidvanias)). The same source gives two room-flow rules. Doors on the z-axis (into the background) never lead to a new district. And when playtesters kept taking the harder branch out of the Wasteland of the Buried Churches, the fix was a **safer-looking stepladder** toward the easier route, not a block on the harder one. Super Metroid's entrance grammar holds up: an "insurmountable stone wall" turns you left. Of two doors in a shaft, the one needing missiles pushes you through the other. And the run-button room locks you in until you learn to run ([Invisible Hand](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-invisible-hand-of-super-metroid)). Lost Crown turns blocked exits into memory hooks, "inaccessible routes often appearing as landmarks". It hides its hardest platforming in an "innocuous" optional room that several level designers re-tested each time a chapter was added ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/prince-persia-lostcrown-metroidvania-gameplay-interview/); [Remap Radio](https://remapradio.com/articles/prince-of-persia-and-the-high-price-of-pants)).

Adam Saltsman's tile-readability rules are the bridge between your black-box collision and painted art. Block in the play area first. Make floors and walls **bright and obvious** and backgrounds dark and receding, then **strip the colour and check that about three values still read**. Add bright edges on solids, with darker bands where background meets them. Put a warm, dark-red outline on solids so they advance against cool backgrounds, and keep one consistent light source. "Gameplay edges win visual priority over light and shadow", and realism comes "a distant second" ([Game Developer, "Sexy Tiles"](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/sexy-tiles-sidescrollers)).

Secrets need a promise and a tell. Pellen wanted players to feel that behind any breakable wall "there could be anything… Or it could be a whole new area with its own boss". He adds: "It's easy to underestimate how good people are at finding stuff." Gibson accepts that missing things "makes it more special for that person" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-makers-of-hollow-knight-are-fine-with-players-missi-1828721254)). Silksong honours that promise at scale. Brown counted **more than 50 secret walls**, and whole regions (Bilewater, the Putrified Ducts) sit behind them. Brown finished the game without visiting Bilewater ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-world-design-of-hollow-knight)). Super Metroid's Charge Beam corridor shows the classic tell: two blocks that "stand out like a sore thumb", a disconnected floor, and a ceiling glimpsed beyond the screen edge ([Invisible Hand](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-invisible-hand-of-super-metroid)). Combat-space evidence is the thinnest in the whole set. No studio publishes arena widths or ceiling heights. The only documented principles are Ori's grey-box test and Sakamoto's claim that Dread's levels give players "everything to help Samus outmaneuver her enemies" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2022/01/metroid-dread-producer-hopes-it-showcased-potential-and-inspires-metroidvania-devs)). Ender Lilies' developers supply the cautionary tale. They piled damaging miasma zones into late areas and admit those were under-tuned and felt unfair rather than challenging ([Game*Spark](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2021/09/05/111615_2.html)). They also removed contact damage in the sequel because "it felt unconvincing to take damage just from bumping into an enemy" ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/ender-magnolia-devs-removed-contact-damage-in-response-to-player-feedback-it-felt-unconvincing-to-take-damage-just-from-bumping-into-an-enemy/)).

## AAA 2D presentation is three bands, relit pixels and an authored camera

Hollow Knight's depth comes from a **foreground, a play layer and a stack of background planes**. PC Gamer lists "objects blocking the screen in the foreground, parallaxed background layers, particle effects, clouds rolling by, and subtle lighting changes as you move through a level" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/hollow-knights-charming-art-sets-the-bar-for-hand-drawn-games); structure per a third-party [Medium analysis](https://medium.com/3d-environmental-art/the-art-of-hollow-knight-f4c05dda3882)). The cost is the warning. Gibson reportedly said that carrying multi-layer parallax across every area **added months**, and that simplifying enemy designs freed him to paint several backgrounds a day (Chinese reprint of an unverified English original; [gcores](https://www.gcores.com/articles/106192)). For pixel art, Dead Cells is the documented pipeline. Backgrounds and decorations are "done in PA and then **redrawn in normal maps**", so a dynamic light system can "change the entire ambience of our levels without having to redraw all of the elements". A torch lights a statue "from the correct direction while respecting its base colours" ([80.lv, Motion Twin](https://80.lv/articles/interview-with-the-developers-of-dead-cells)). Its characters render at about 50 px tall without antialiasing, using per-frame normals and a toon shader. Motion Twin admits it "still haven't found any solution for flickering pixels" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)). Blasphemous's Colinet warns that being "very 'purist' about the pixel art style… became a real problem because we were limited by" it ([Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/blasphemous-designer-purist-approach-games-pixel-art-style-1238370)). No developer source gives per-layer scroll factors, fog values or hazard-colour rules for any of the benchmarks.

Godot 4.7 documents every needed piece, with a few traps. **Parallax2D** replaced ParallaxBackground "prior to 4.3". The official reference speeds run Sky 0.1, high clouds 0.2, low clouds 0.3, hills 0.5 and forest 0.7. Textures must start at (0,0) and be at least viewport-sized before you set `repeat_size`, and "`repeat_size` and `region_rect` do not take scaling into account" ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/2d/2d_parallax.html)). For lighting, **CanvasModulate** sets the biome's ambient. Lights only brighten without it, and the clear colour never receives light. Shadow filter None is "fastest, suited to pixel art". Lights are computed **at the Viewport's pixel resolution**, so under your `viewport` stretch mode at 480×270 they are already game-pixel sized. Occluders can be painted per tile in a TileSet occlusion layer ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/2d/2d_lights_and_shadows.html)). **HDR 2D** works on Forward+ and Mobile, and gives per-object bloom through Modulate values above 1. It switches the 2D renderer to linear space, so shader colour textures need `source_color` or they wash out. **`Canvas Max Layer`** keeps the UI and a vignette out of the glow. The Compatibility renderer hides glow's Levels, Strength, Blend Mode and Map ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html)). A forum user saw a saturation shift with Canvas background plus HDR under the Mobile renderer and ended up baking glow into the art ([Godot Forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/how-do-i-make-sure-the-hdr-glow-bloom-effect-doesnt-get-applied-to-certain-canvas-layers/106258)). On terrain, a TileSet separates physics, occlusion and custom-data layers, such as `damage_per_second` per tile. Version 4.7 adds a **2D Scene Paint Mode** (B) for scattering decoration without a tilemap, `one_way_collision_direction` on CollisionShape2D, and `GradientTexture2D` `FILL_CONIC` ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/2d/using_tilesets.html); [Godot 4.7 release](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.7/)).

The camera has the best non-developer reference of the whole field: Itay Keren's "Scroll Back". It catalogues a **camera window one jump high** (Rastan), **platform-snapping** that holds the vertical position during jumps and snaps on landing (Super Mario World), forward focus about **25% of screen width** ahead (Defender), speed-scaled focus (Cave Story), and per-region camera zones and boss attractors (Donkey Kong Country, Mushroom 11). It also warns that projected focus "works poorly for jumps and landings" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/scroll-back-the-theory-and-practice-of-cameras-in-side-scrollers)). Godot's Camera2D defaults to drag margins of 0.2 and smoothing off. `limit_smoothed` needs smoothing on. `get_screen_center_position()` is the true on-screen centre, and `reset_smoothing()` prevents a sweep across the map after a teleport ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_camera2d.html)). Physics interpolation fixes jitter from mismatched tick rates, but it interpolates teleports and adds input lag ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/jitter_stutter.html)). Integer scaling has a practical consequence for 480×270: it fills 1080p (4×) and 4K (8×) exactly, but **1440p gets 5× (2400×1350) with black bars**, and plain Fullscreen's 1-px height reduction can drop the scale factor, so Exclusive Fullscreen is advised ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/multiple_resolutions.html)). For impact, Sakurai defines hit-stop as "the frozen frames that emphasize the power of the hit". Smash caps its duration and gives projectiles less ([Source Gaming translation](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/)).

## Design rules for the Seeker's world

The rules below translate the evidence into a starting spec. The **[Sourced]** tag means a cited source directly supports the rule. **[Recommendation]** means the writer's synthesis. **[Calc]** means a number derived from your `src/player/player_config.tres` (gravity 900, jump 330, double jump 320, fall gravity ×1.5, apex hang ×0.55, run 140, dash 370 for 0.25 s, dash-jump keep 0.85, wall jump 380, grapple range 200, hitbox 10×22). The calculations come from a 60 Hz simulation that ignores corner correction and ledge nudge, so measure them again in `movement_test_room` before you lock any gate. Your project already runs Forward+, `viewport` stretch, `integer` scale and nearest filtering, so everything below fits as configured.

### Key order: put the low-leak keys early and the double jump in the middle

Your current build gates only the double jump and the grapple (`PlayerState`), and the brief has both found in region 1. The sources argue for separating them. The double jump invalidates every 60–115 px ledge in the world at once. Every benchmark gives it mid-to-late: 4th of 6 in Hollow Knight, 7th of 9 in Silksong, 14th of 22 in Dread **[Sourced]**. The grapple only works on anchors you place, so it opens exactly the locks you author, which is Dread's "few locks per key" in its purest form. That makes it the safest early key **[Recommendation]**.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the effort should go. Your pillars already name the right goals. The sources show the leading teams spend most of their effort on the **player's memory**, and less on locks. Team Cherry cut a biome for memory reasons. Ubisoft built a capped screenshot system after forgetting its own objectives. MercurySteam moved a scan tool later because it made remembering unnecessary. Nine Sols, your main reference, is criticised most for its map and its spoke-like linearity. So the safest way to take its transit-and-parry identity is to pair it with Hollow Knight's cross-connections and a stronger map than Nine Sols has.

For the Seeker the decisive finding is mechanical. The dash-jump already carries about 165 px and wall jumps chain without limit, so horizontal gaps and walled shafts are weak locks. The grapple anchor is the strongest gate the kit has. The double jump is a hinge, so place it at the moment the map should open. Building the measured reach table into `movement_test_room`, and re-checking every gate against it whenever movement is tuned, will prevent more broken progression than any other single practice in this report.
